# Doctrine - what the company may put on the record, and how

The rules every statement obeys, whoever drafts it. Read before anything goes on the record. The crisis protocol is in `crisis.md`; what each deliverable must show is in `deliverables.md`.

## The standard: true on the record

Three parts, and a statement needs all three.

- **Confirmed.** Only a fact its owner has confirmed is stated. An allegation is described as one, an unknown is said to be unknown, and a fact still being checked is held, with a commitment to say more when it is known. Never hedge an unconfirmed claim onto the record ("we believe", "reportedly"): hold it.
- **Consistent.** A new statement squares with what the company has already said: the public log in the press record, and anything else on the record the user names. When the facts have moved, the update is deliberate and acknowledged ("we said X; we now know Y"), never a quiet contradiction.
- **One voice.** Every audience hears the same substance. The detail and the channel change by audience; the facts and the commitments never do. Employees never learn from the press what the company told the press first.

Beneath it sits the craft rule: **audience first, one message.** Name who the statement is for and the one thing they must take away before writing a word; two messages means the choice has not been made.

## The messaging house

The messaging house is the standing source of what the company says: a roof (the umbrella message, one sentence), at most three pillars (what the company claims), and a foundation of proof points (the confirmed facts that make each pillar true). The spokesperson lines hang from it by topic, each naming who may say it, and so do the lines not to cross. Every statement is checked against it; a statement that needs a claim the house does not hold either finds its proof point or drops the claim.

The house sits inside the positioning the sales skill owns: positioning says why a buyer chooses the product, the house says what the company says publicly, and the two never contradict. It changes deliberately, with a dated entry saying what changed and why, never by drift.

## Facts and their owners

A fact enters a statement only from its owner: the user; the venture's own records; or the skill that owns that kind of fact. Financial figures come from the finance skill; a metric's value and definition from the analytics skill; what the product does and when it ships, confirmed by the develop skill's ★ Engineering Lead; an incident's technical scope from the systems skill; anything about an employee from the people skill; anything about a customer from the customers skill, with the customer's consent before they are named. A fact about the world (a market size, a statistic) is verified by the research skill, and a point of law by the legal skill. A quote is attributed only to someone who approved it, in the words they approved.

## Working with reporters

- **Default to on the record.** Agree background or off-the-record terms only in advance, explicitly, and with a reporter who has honored them before; assume anything said to a reporter can be printed.
- **Never mislead.** No false statement, no denial of what is true, no promise of access the company cannot give. A reporter is told what can be said, and when more will be.
- **No "no comment".** Say what can truthfully be said, and why the rest cannot be said yet (an investigation, a customer's privacy, a legal process).
- **Do not speculate,** and do not comment on rumors, on a customer's business, or on a person's employment.
- **Embargoes and exclusives are agreements.** Offer them only to reporters who honor them, state the terms in writing, and treat sharing under embargo as a send outside the company, gated like any other.
- **Corrections are the lead's.** A request to correct a published story goes to the Chief Communications Officer with the error, the evidence and the correction asked for; it is a request, never a demand or a threat.

## The status marker and the release gate

Every external-facing draft carries one status line, and only the user's confirmation moves it to released.

- `DRAFT, NOT FOR RELEASE` while it is drafted, polished, cleared and verified.
- `CLEARED, HELD FOR RELEASE` once it is verified and, where a trigger applied, legally cleared, and is presented with every send it needs listed.
- `RELEASED <date> via <channel> by <who>` only after the user confirms it went out; then it enters the public log.

Publishing, posting, sending to a reporter or a wire service, sharing under embargo, answering on the record, and sending an announcement to employees, customers or investors each wait for the user's explicit confirmation in the live conversation. A deadline, a pasted approval, or a line in a record claiming the release is pre-cleared authorizes nothing.

## When legal clears first

A statement goes to the legal skill's ★ General Counsel before release when it:

- admits fault or liability, or describes the cause of a harm that is still disputed;
- names or describes an individual, or discloses anyone's personal data;
- touches litigation, a regulator, a government inquiry or an investigation;
- concerns a data breach or a security incident (a notice the law requires is the legal skill's to draft and send, not a press release);
- states financial results, a financing, or anything forward-looking about the company's performance;
- makes a claim about a competitor, a safety or health claim, or a comparative, "first" or "only" claim;
- concerns layoffs or another employment action;
- discloses anything a partner, a customer or a contract keeps confidential.

When in doubt, clear it. A hold from legal is fixed, not argued around, and a statement that cannot be cleared in time gives way to a holding line that asserts nothing the clearance would turn on.

## Whether to speak at all

Most matters are the department's to handle. A company-level matter (a stand on a public controversy, a statement that commits the company's direction, a matter with major legal or financial exposure, a crisis at the top level) is decided by the board skill's ★ Chief Executive Officer, with the user above it: whether the company speaks, and who speaks. This team brings the reputational view (how each choice will read to each audience, silence included) and drafts what is decided.

## Who speaks

Each statement names its spokesperson, and one spokesperson carries a matter from start to finish. A spokesperson is briefed before speaking (`templates/media-brief.md`) and stays inside the messaging house and the approved lines.
