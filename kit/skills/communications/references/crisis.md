# Crisis - triage, hold, sequence, update

How the team runs an issue or a crisis. Read it the moment one is live. The deliverables and their completion evidence are in `deliverables.md`; the scaffold is `templates/crisis-plan.md`. The governing rule: the company speaks once, true and on time, not first at any cost. A wrong word in a crisis costs more than a silent hour, and a promised update that never comes costs more than either.

## Triage: severity and trajectory

Classify before drafting. Severity is how bad it is; trajectory is how fast it is spreading. Take the higher level either one points to.

| Level | Severity | Trajectory | Response |
|-------|----------|------------|----------|
| **1, issue** | a complaint, a critical post or review, a contained error; no one harmed | flat: one channel, no pickup | managed quietly: monitor it, keep a reserve holding line ready, and make no public statement unless asked |
| **2, serious** | real harm to customers, their money or their trust, or a reporter already asking | rising: several channels, or one influential voice | a holding statement drafted, cleared and ready; the stakeholder sequence staged; the lead and the legal skill engaged |
| **3, crisis** | harm to people's safety, data, money or rights; a regulator, law enforcement or litigation involved; or a threat to the company itself | fast: mainstream pickup, trending, or a regulator asking | the full response plan; the board skill's ★ Chief Executive Officer and the user told at once; whether the company speaks, and who speaks, is the board's call |

A level moves up when the trajectory rises, a new audience joins (the press, a regulator, a large customer), or a confirmed fact turns out worse; it moves down only when the facts are confirmed and the spread has stopped. When the severity cannot be judged at all, ask for the scope and the spread before drafting anything but a reserve line.

## The fact line

Before any statement: what is confirmed, what is alleged and what is unknown, each with the owner who will confirm it and when. Only confirmed facts enter a statement; an allegation is described as one, and an unknown is said to be under investigation. The technical facts of an outage or a breach come from the systems skill, whose Chief Information Security Officer carries the security-risk line to the user; who is affected comes from the customers skill; anything about an employee comes from the people skill.

## The holding statement

The first statement acknowledges; it does not explain. It:

1. acknowledges what is known, plainly;
2. shows care for the people affected, without admitting what is not established;
3. says what the company is doing now;
4. commits to the next update by a named time, and keeps it, even when there is nothing new to say;
5. asserts nothing unconfirmed, speculates about nothing, blames no one, and never says "no comment".

A statement that would admit liability, name a person or could not be retracted goes to the Chief Communications Officer and the legal skill's ★ General Counsel first. The pre-cleared holding statements in the standing crisis plan save the clock: adapt the nearest one rather than drafting from nothing.

## The stakeholder sequence

One message everywhere; the order decides who hears it first. The default, adjusted to the matter:

1. **The people affected**, directly and first, with what they should do (customers through the customers skill's channels).
2. **Employees**, before they read it anywhere else, with what to say if they are asked.
3. **Regulators and authorities**, where the law requires notice; the legal skill owns that notice and its legal clock, which runs regardless of this order.
4. **The board and investors**, through the board skill.
5. **Customers and partners who are not directly affected.**
6. **The press and the public**, with the holding statement, then the updates.

Each audience has an owner, a channel, a time and a spokesperson, and every send in the sequence waits for the user's confirmation. When an audience is likely to learn early (a public filing, a reporter already asking), the sequence is compressed, never inverted: the people affected and the employees hear before or with the public.

## The update loop

Keep the promised cadence. Each update says what has changed in the fact line, what the company is doing, and when it will speak next. Log every release with its time, audience and channel. Correct the company's own errors fast and plainly.

## After it closes

Close the issue on the record once the facts are settled and the remedy is done, then review it: what was said, what held, what did not, and how fast. Fold what was learned into the standing crisis plan (its triggers, its pre-cleared statements, its sequence), and record every release in the public log.
