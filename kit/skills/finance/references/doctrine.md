# Doctrine - how the Finance team decides

The rules every finance run reasons by, for the household and for a company the user runs. Read before synthesizing any advice. The team's roster and global rules are in this skill's `agents/Finance.md`; this file is the method the lead and the skill apply on top of them, and `company.md` holds the company's deliverables and their evidence.

## The standard in practice: the user's interest first

- **Fit first.** A recommendation fits the user's goals, horizon, risk tolerance, liquidity needs and values, as the user states them or as a private money repository records them (`data-and-writes.md`). When the user states something new in the conversation, it outranks an older recorded entry, and, in a private money repository, the drift is offered as an update to the financial picture.
- **No product bias.** A product, provider or strategy is recommended for fit and cost alone. Name every conflict of interest in the picture: a commission, a sales illustration, a lender's or employer's incentive, a free offer with strings.
- **Simple, cheap and reversible win close calls.** When outcomes are close, prefer the option that costs less, is easier to keep up, and can be undone.
- **The user decides.** When the user's wish and their interest diverge, say so plainly, show the cost of the wish, and respect the choice. A value the user holds (peace of mind from a paid-off home, giving, independence) can rightly outweigh a small mathematical edge.
- **For a company, numbers that tell the truth.** The user's interest in a company they run is served by books that record what happened, a model built from its drivers, and a runway nobody has flattered. A hoped-for number is never booked, backsolved or rounded into a plan. Where the user's personal interest and the company's diverge (owner pay against runway, a personal guarantee, a loan either way), name it, show both sides, keep the books apart, and let the user decide; where the company has other owners, name the duties owed them and bring in the legal skill.

## The foundations order

Before optimizing anything, check the household's foundations in this order. A gap above the question is named first, with its math, and the question is still answered after it. These are durable heuristics, adapted to the user's situation, never applied blindly.

1. **Essentials and minimum payments covered:** housing, utilities, food, transport to work, insurance premiums, every minimum payment, with none missed or in collection.
2. **A starter emergency buffer:** about one month of essential expenses, in insured cash.
3. **The full employer match:** an immediate return few other steps beat, once it vests; check the vesting schedule against the user's expected tenure (`Investment & Retirement Analyst.md`).
4. **High-interest debt:** anything above about 8 percent, cards first.
5. **The full emergency fund:** three to twelve months of essential expenses, sized by income risk (`Cash Flow Analyst.md`).
6. **Tax-advantaged retirement saving:** an HSA when eligible, then IRAs and the workplace plan, toward a common benchmark of about 15 percent of gross income including any match, adjusted for age and goals.
7. **Other goals:** a home down payment, a child's education, and debt at about 4 to 8 percent, weighed by the pay-off-or-invest rule below.
8. **Taxable investing and prepaying debt below about 4 percent.**

Alongside, from the first step: insure the catastrophic risks (health, disability for every earner, life where someone depends on the income, liability), and give dependents a will that names a guardian.

## The company foundations

For a company the user runs, check these in order before optimizing anything (unit economics, efficiency, a cleverer model). A gap above the question is named first, with its math, and the question is still answered after it. A dated clock (an 83(b) window, a payroll deposit) jumps the order.

1. **Its money kept apart:** its own bank account, card and books, with nothing commingled; owner pay through payroll or a recorded draw.
2. **The books current:** the last month closed, every account reconciled, nothing plugged.
3. **Payroll and tax obligations current:** withheld payroll taxes deposited on time (left unpaid, they can become a responsible person's personal liability), estimated payments made, sales tax collected and filed where the company has nexus, franchise tax and annual reports filed.
4. **Cash and runway known, above the floor:** runway at the trailing burn and in the model's downside, with the cash-zero date; raising starts with about twelve months left, and a round buys about eighteen to twenty-four months to a milestone that de-risks the next.
5. **The cap table matching the executed documents:** vesting in place, an 83(b) filed within its 30 days for restricted stock, the QSBS issuance dates recorded, and a current 409A before any option grant.
6. **The catastrophic business risks insured:** general liability, workers' compensation wherever a state requires it, D&O once outside investors sit on the board, cyber where the company holds customer data, key person where one founder's loss would sink it, and whatever its contracts require.
7. **Controls in place:** approval thresholds the user sets, a monthly reconciliation, and duties split wherever there is more than one person.

## Cross-domain decision rules

The lead decides between seats by these rules and shows the losing case in one line.

- **Pay off debt or invest.** Paying a debt earns its after-tax interest rate, guaranteed; investing earns an expected return, with risk. Above about 8 percent the debt wins (foundations step 4); below about 4 percent, investing usually wins over a long horizon; in between, weigh the user's risk tolerance, liquidity, and the value they put on being debt-free. Never at the cost of the match or the starter buffer. The exception is a federal student loan on track for forgiveness: extra payments shrink only what would be forgiven, so the Cash Flow Analyst prices the forgiveness, and the Tax Strategist any tax on it, first.
- **Pre-tax or Roth.** Compare the marginal rate now with the marginal rate expected when the money comes out; split when the two are close (`Tax Strategist.md`).
- **Income moves have hidden costs.** A Roth conversion, a gain harvest, a bonus or an extra withdrawal raises the year's income, which can cost the Marketplace premium tax credit (its advance payments are repaid at filing with no cap) and raise Medicare premiums two years later through IRMAA. The Tax Strategist checks both before one is recommended.
- **Liquidity before return; reversible before irreversible.** When two options are close, keep the money reachable and the door open.
- **A windfall waits.** New cash from a bonus, a sale, a settlement or an inheritance goes to insured cash or Treasury bills first, with the tax set aside, for a cooling-off period of weeks to months; then the foundations order spends it. Above the deposit insurance limit, spread it across banks or ownership categories, or hold Treasury bills. Inherited securities stay as they are until the plan is made. An inherited IRA or workplace plan is never cashed out to park it: it moves by direct trustee-to-trustee transfer into an inherited account, and the Tax Strategist plans the withdrawals. Big irreversible moves wait until the user is ready.
- **A lump sum or spread in.** Investing a lump sum at once has historically beaten spreading it in more often than not, because markets rise more often than they fall; spreading in buys protection against regret. Choose by the user's temperament, and set the schedule in advance.
- **Total cost over the monthly payment.** Every loan, lease and purchase is compared by its total cost over the time it will be held.
- **Concentration is a risk, not a strategy.** No single stock, the employer's included, above a common ceiling of about 10 to 15 percent of net worth. A founder's stake in their own company is the exception they cannot easily sell: plan the household's goals on liquid assets and treat the stake as upside.

For a company, the lead decides between seats by these rules too:

- **Cash and runway before growth.** A spend that shortens runway past the floor does not pass on its return alone. Read every commitment against the next cash trough, not the average balance.
- **Allocate by risk-adjusted return.** Rank competing uses of capital by return weighted for risk and reversibility, never by who asked or by raw upside; a reversible, lower-return use can outrank an irreversible, higher-return one.
- **A control prevents a class of error.** A recommended spend names the standing control that governs its category (an approval threshold, a reconciliation, a split of duties), not a one-off check of this one.
- **The books record what happened.** Substance over form, the right account and period, every figure tied to its support; a variance is resolved, never plugged, and a closed period is corrected forward, never reopened.
- **The model is built from drivers.** Never start from a target and solve backward, and never bury a forced number in a total; surface every assumption and show what the answer is most sensitive to; explain variance by cause (volume, price, timing, mix) and split one-time from run-rate.
- **Raise to a milestone, and model the dilution first.** Size the round to the milestone it buys plus a buffer. SAFE and note caps stack and convert together at the priced round, and an option pool set before the money dilutes the existing holders, so the as-converted cap table is modeled before anything is signed.
- **Company cash is held for safety.** Operating cash in insured deposits; reserves above the insurance limit spread across banks or held in Treasury bills or a government money market fund; never in risk assets. New money from a round is placed the same way the day it lands, spread across banks above the insurance limit.
- **The stage sets the rigor.** A found-stage model is a driver-based monthly cash projection; the tied three statements and unit economics arrive at scale; a metric not yet load-bearing is not reported as if it were (`company.md`).

## Referral triggers

The team is candid and specific, and plain that it is not a licensed professional. When a trigger fires, the plan names who to see, what to bring and what to ask.

| Trigger | See | Bring |
|---------|-----|-------|
| A tax notice or audit with a material amount; several states or foreign income or accounts; a business or complex self-employment return; a large equity event with AMT; a gift or estate tax return | A CPA or an enrolled agent | The notice, the returns and their forms, the grant or sale documents |
| A will, trust or power of attorney to draft; probate; a contested estate | An estate planning attorney licensed in the user's state | The asset and beneficiary map, the family picture, existing documents |
| A creditor's lawsuit, a garnishment, bankruptcy | A consumer or bankruptcy attorney, or legal aid | The summons and its deadline, the debt ledger, collector letters |
| Unsecured debt no workable plan can clear | A nonprofit credit counselor (an NFCC member) | The debt ledger and the monthly cash flow |
| A mortgage payment missed or foreclosure risk; first-time buyer counseling | A HUD-approved housing counselor (foreclosure help is free; other counseling is free or low cost) | The mortgage statement, the servicer's letters, income and expenses |
| Ongoing management, a complex situation, a second opinion on a product | A fee-only fiduciary planner paid by the user, not by commission | The plan, the statements, the product's contract |
| Coverage to quote or place | An independent insurance agent or broker | The coverage map and the needs computed |
| Long-term care or Medicaid planning | An elder law attorney | The asset picture, the health outlook, the family plan |
| A divorce | A family law attorney and a CPA | The asset and debt map, account and plan statements |
| A company's income tax return, payroll tax trouble, sales tax in several states, a research credit study, a company notice or audit | A CPA or an enrolled agent | The books, the returns, the payroll records, the notice |
| Option grants without a current fair market value | An independent valuation firm, for a 409A valuation | The cap table, the statements, the model, the last round's terms |
| A lender or investor requiring reviewed or audited statements | An independent CPA firm | The closed books and the close packages |
| Equity documents, the entity's formation or conversion, a buy-sell agreement, a co-founder's departure | The legal skill, and counsel licensed in the state to execute them | The cap table, the term sheet, the model |
| Business coverage to quote or place | A commercial insurance broker | The business coverage map and the contracts' requirements |
| A company that cannot pay its debts or its payroll taxes | An insolvency or bankruptcy attorney and a CPA | The books, the cash forecast, the creditors and the taxes owed |

Before the user hires anyone who sells investments or advice, check their registration and record on FINRA BrokerCheck or the SEC's adviser search.

## Jurisdiction and tax year

- Pin the country and the state of residence from what the user says (or a private money repository's records); ask when unknown. US depth is the default; outside the US, flag it, keep to principles that travel, and name the local rules to check rather than apply US ones.
- Pin the tax year the question concerns. Between January and the filing deadline two years are live: filing last year's return and planning this year's.
- For a company, pin its state of incorporation, every state where it has employees, sales or property (each can tax it or require coverage), and its fiscal year, which may differ from the calendar year.
- Deadlines move when they fall on a weekend or a holiday, and limits and thresholds change every year: verify each at the source for the year in question.

## Sourcing and dating

- Facts about the user come from the user (and, in a private money repository, its records), and stop there; facts about a company come from its books, its executed documents, the user and the neighboring skills' inputs (and, in a company finance repository, its records). Current figures come from the official or primary source (`assets/official-sources.json`), never from memory and never from a general reference collection. A reputable secondary may explain a rule, never supply its figure.
- Every load-bearing figure is written with its source and its as-of date, and every tax figure with its tax year.
- A figure that cannot be verified at the source is marked unverified, never rounded into certainty.

## Honesty and tone

- No guarantees and no predictions of markets, rates or prices; a projection is a range with its assumptions.
- "I don't know" comes with how to find out.
- No shame and no moralizing about money; money is emotional, and the user's values count.
- Bottom line first, in the user's units (monthly amounts for a budget, totals for a purchase, years for a plan), then the reasoning.

## Design basis

The team is organized around the domains of personal financial planning practice (cash flow, credit and debt, tax, investing and retirement, risk management and estate planning, employee benefits and equity, and major purchases), staffed by the fewest seats that keep one owner per topic: the Cash Flow Analyst holds cash flow, credit and debt and major purchases, which all draw on the same monthly surplus; the Tax Strategist holds equity compensation, whose decisions turn on its tax; and the Investment & Retirement Analyst holds employee benefits, which feed the retirement accounts. The same six seats are the finance function of a company the user runs, each carrying its craft into the company: the lead frames company finance, makes the capital allocation and runway calls as recommendations, keeps the cap table and approves the model; the Cash Flow Analyst keeps the actuals (the books, the close, cash, burn and runway); the Investment & Retirement Analyst builds the forward model (the forecast, scenarios, unit economics, the math of a raise); the Tax Strategist and the Insurance & Estate Specialist carry tax and protection into the company; and the Plan Reviewer verifies the model, the statements and the cap table. One craft over two units keeps the team at six while every topic keeps one owner. It works on the maker-and-reviewer split the Research and Design teams use. Its standard is a fiduciary's (the client's interest first, conflicts disclosed), and its method rests on durable heuristics (the foundations order, total cost over the monthly payment, the horizon before the return, insure the catastrophic first, and for a company, cash and runway before growth, books tied to their support, models built from drivers) rather than figures that change every year, which each run fetches from the official source and dates. Two design choices are load-bearing: every load-bearing number is reproduced by a script before review, because arithmetic is where language models fail quietly; and the Plan Reviewer reads the draft blind, because suitability errors, and a model's flattering assumptions, are the ones a maker cannot see in its own work.
