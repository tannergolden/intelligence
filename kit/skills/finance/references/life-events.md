# Life events - the money checklist for each

A life event runs as a deep plan. Read the event's entry before dispatching: it names the seats to convene, the time-critical items to date first, and the first moves. Every window below is the usual US one, varies by plan and state, and is verified at the source before it is quoted; date each one into `Memory/Dates.md`.

## Rules for every event

- **Time-critical items first.** Date every window before any optimizing; a missed window usually cannot be reopened.
- **Big, irreversible decisions wait** until the Founder is ready, most of all after a death, a divorce or a windfall.
- **Beneficiaries, coverage and the budget** are checked at every event, because each one changes at least one of them.

## A new job or an offer

- Seats: Elliot (the offer on total compensation, the new benefits, the enrollment window), Harriet (a new W-4), Graham (the old workplace plan: leave it, or roll it to the new plan or an IRA by direct rollover, checking first with Harriet for net unrealized appreciation on employer stock, the age-55 rule, and the pro-rata effect an IRA balance has on a backdoor Roth; only one IRA-to-IRA 60-day rollover is allowed in any 12 months, counted from the earlier distribution, and trustee-to-trustee transfers and conversions do not count), Clara (any gap in coverage between jobs).
- Time-critical: the new employer's enrollment window, often 30 days from the start date.

## A layoff or job loss

- Seats: Lucille first (cut to essentials, the emergency fund's runway in months, unemployment insurance and any means-tested benefits now in reach), Elliot (severance terms, unvested equity, option exercise windows, the plan's COBRA dates), Clara (health coverage: she decides COBRA or a Marketplace plan, priced net of the premium tax credit), Owen (hardship programs before any payment is missed), Harriet (withholding on severance; unemployment benefits are taxable).
- Time-critical: file for unemployment at once; COBRA or a Marketplace plan decided inside the Marketplace's 60 days from losing job coverage (COBRA's own election window runs 60 days from its notice, and an elected COBRA is retroactive), since leaving COBRA early opens no special enrollment period except at Open Enrollment; option exercise windows, often 90 days, and an ISO not exercised within three months of leaving is taxed as an NSO; a workplace plan loan can come due faster after separation, and an unpaid balance is taxed unless rolled over by the return's due date, extensions included.

## Marriage

- Seats: Harriet (filing status and withholding for both), Clara (beneficiaries, health plan choice, life and disability needs), Elliot (two benefit packages compared), Lucille (how the household will share money), Owen (credit stays individual; any joint account or loan is a deliberate choice).
- Time-critical: the health plan's special enrollment window, often 30 days at work and 60 on the Marketplace. A name change goes to the SSA first, then to every other record.
- Update the will, the powers of attorney and the health care directive.

## A divorce

- Refer first: a family law attorney and a CPA; the team supports, it never negotiates.
- Seats: Owen (joint debts and accounts, separated cleanly), Graham (dividing retirement accounts; a workplace plan needs a qualified domestic relations order), Harriet (filing status, set by marital status on December 31), Clara (coverage, and beneficiaries changed when the court allows, since changes can be restricted while the case is open and a divorce does not change an ERISA plan's form), Lucille (a one-income budget).

## A new child

- Seats: Clara (the health plan the child joins, life and disability needs, a will naming a guardian, beneficiaries and contingents through a trust or a custodian, never the child by name), Elliot (adding the child at work inside the plan's window, the dependent care FSA), Harriet (credits, compared with the dependent care FSA, and the child's Social Security number), Graham (education savings), Lucille (childcare in the budget).
- A government-seeded child account, if the IRS offers one for the child's birth year, is an IRA-type account, not education savings: Harriet confirms eligibility and dates its election at IRS.gov, and Graham weighs it separately.
- Time-critical: the health plan's special enrollment window, often 30 days at work; any election for a government-seeded child account.

## A death in the family, or an inheritance

- Pause: no irreversible decision for weeks to months unless a deadline forces it.
- Time-critical: a qualified disclaimer, if any heir may want one, within nine months of the death and before accepting the asset (with the estate attorney); a surviving spouse's portability election, made on an estate tax return filed on time even when no tax is due; the owner's required distribution for the year of death, when the owner had begun them and had not taken it; life insurance, pension and SSA survivor claims.
- Seats: Clara (life insurance and beneficiary claims; an estate attorney for probate), Harriet (inherited retirement accounts: most heirs other than a spouse must empty one by the end of the tenth year after the death and, when the owner had already begun required distributions, take one every year in between; a minor child of the owner, a disabled or chronically ill heir, or one not more than ten years younger follows other rules, fetched at IRS.gov; the step-up in basis on inherited taxable assets, which retirement accounts do not get; estate and inheritance tax thresholds, federal and state), Owen (the estate, not the heirs, generally pays the deceased's debts: check before paying any from personal funds), Graham (placing the inheritance, and survivor benefits from the SSA when a spouse dies), Lucille (the household budget).
- Never cash out an inherited retirement account to park the money: it moves by direct trustee-to-trustee transfer into an inherited account first.
- Order several certified death certificates; notify the SSA.

## A windfall (a bonus, an equity sale, a settlement, a large gift)

- New cash goes to insured cash or Treasury bills with the tax set aside (Harriet), spread across banks above the deposit insurance limit; wait, then spend it by the foundations order in `doctrine.md` (the lead, with Owen and Graham). Inherited securities stay as they are until the plan is made, and an inherited retirement account is never cashed out to park it.
- A bonus or a sale in a year the household buys Marketplace coverage is checked against the premium tax credit first (Harriet).

## Moving to another state

- Seats: Harriet (residency and domicile, part-year returns, the new state's tax), Henry (housing), Clara (insurance re-quoted for the new state, and estate documents checked against its law).
- File the change of address with the IRS and update every account.

## Buying or selling a home

- Seats: Henry leads the purchase, then Owen (the mortgage), Clara (homeowners and title coverage), Harriet (the home-sale exclusion when selling).

## Going self-employed or starting a company

- Seats: Harriet (quarterly estimated payments, self-employment tax, and the premium tax credit's income limits when the household buys Marketplace coverage), Lucille (irregular income and a nine-to-twelve-month reserve), Clara (health coverage priced net of the premium tax credit, disability and liability coverage), Graham (a solo 401(k) or SEP IRA), Elliot (what is forfeited at the old employer).
- The venture itself is `board`'s, staffed by AR's Finance & Accounting department; keep the business and personal books separate from day one.

## Approaching retirement

- Seats: Graham (the drawdown plan, Social Security timing, a pension's lump-sum-or-annuity choice), Harriet (conversions in low-income years, checked against the Medicare income surcharge and, before 65 on a Marketplace plan, the premium tax credit's income limits; required distributions at the age the current rules set), Clara (Medicare enrollment with the state's free SHIP counseling, and long-term care), Lucille (the retirement budget).
- Time-critical: Medicare's initial enrollment period, the seven months around the 65th birthday, since enrolling late can carry lifelong penalties; Part B delayed only while group coverage from current employment pays first, which the plan confirms, because at an employer with fewer than 20 employees Medicare usually pays first, so enroll in Part B when first eligible; never delayed on COBRA or a retiree plan; the Medigap open enrollment window; HSA contributions stopped six months before applying for Medicare or Social Security after 65, because Part A is backdated.

## A serious illness, an injury or a disability

- Seats: Clara (disability and health claims), Elliot (employer leave and benefits), Graham (Social Security disability, which pays only after a waiting period, so apply early), Lucille (an essentials budget, and means-tested benefits if income falls), Owen (hardship programs before any payment is missed); confirm the powers of attorney and the health care directive are in place.

## Identity theft or fraud

- Seats: Owen (freeze all three bureaus, fraud alerts, the recovery plan at IdentityTheft.gov, disputes for fraudulent accounts), Harriet (an IRS Identity Protection PIN, and the IRS identity theft process if a false return was filed).
- Change compromised passwords; the vault records only that it was done and where credentials live.

## Caring for an aging parent

- Seats: Clara (long-term care and an elder law attorney), Harriet (dependent status and credits), Lucille (the care costs in the budget).
