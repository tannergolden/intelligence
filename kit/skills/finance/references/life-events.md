# Life events - the money checklist for each

A life event runs as a deep plan. Read the event's entry before dispatching: it names the seats to convene, the time-critical items to date first, and the first moves. Every window below is the usual US one, varies by plan and state, and is verified at the source before it is quoted; date each one in the delivery (and, in a private money repository, in its deadline list, per `data-and-writes.md`).

## Rules for every event

- **Time-critical items first.** Date every window before any optimizing; a missed window usually cannot be reopened.
- **Big, irreversible decisions wait** until the user is ready, most of all after a death, a divorce or a windfall.
- **Beneficiaries, coverage and the budget** are checked at every event, because each one changes at least one of them.

## A new job or an offer

- Seats: The Investment & Retirement Analyst (the offer on total compensation, the new benefits and the enrollment window, and the old workplace plan: leave it, or roll it to the new plan or an IRA by direct rollover, checking first with the Tax Strategist for net unrealized appreciation on employer stock, the age-55 rule, and the pro-rata effect an IRA balance has on a backdoor Roth; only one IRA-to-IRA 60-day rollover is allowed in any 12 months, counted from the earlier distribution, and trustee-to-trustee transfers and conversions do not count), the Tax Strategist (a new W-4, and the equity in the offer valued with its vesting and tax), the Insurance & Estate Specialist (any gap in coverage between jobs).
- Time-critical: the new employer's enrollment window, often 30 days from the start date.

## A layoff or job loss

- Seats: The Cash Flow Analyst first (cut to essentials, the emergency fund's runway in months, unemployment insurance and any means-tested benefits now in reach, and hardship programs before any payment is missed), the Investment & Retirement Analyst (severance terms, the unvested match, a plan loan, the workplace plan's rollover, the plan's COBRA dates), the Insurance & Estate Specialist (health coverage: she decides COBRA or a Marketplace plan, priced net of the premium tax credit), the Tax Strategist (unvested equity and option exercise windows; withholding on severance; unemployment benefits are taxable).
- Time-critical: file for unemployment at once; COBRA or a Marketplace plan decided inside the Marketplace's 60 days from losing job coverage (COBRA's own election window runs 60 days from its notice, and an elected COBRA is retroactive), since leaving COBRA early opens no special enrollment period except at Open Enrollment; option exercise windows, often 90 days, and an ISO not exercised within three months of leaving is taxed as an NSO; a workplace plan loan can come due faster after separation, and an unpaid balance is taxed unless rolled over by the return's due date, extensions included.

## Marriage

- Seats: The Tax Strategist (filing status and withholding for both), the Insurance & Estate Specialist (beneficiaries, health plan choice, life and disability needs), the Investment & Retirement Analyst (two benefit packages compared), the Cash Flow Analyst (how the household will share money; credit stays individual, and any joint account or loan is a deliberate choice).
- Time-critical: the health plan's special enrollment window, often 30 days at work and 60 on the Marketplace. A name change goes to the SSA first, then to every other record.
- Update the will, the powers of attorney and the health care directive.

## A divorce

- Refer first: a family law attorney and a CPA; the team supports, it never negotiates.
- Seats: The Cash Flow Analyst (a one-income budget, and joint debts and accounts separated cleanly), the Investment & Retirement Analyst (dividing retirement accounts; a workplace plan needs a qualified domestic relations order), the Tax Strategist (filing status, set by marital status on December 31), the Insurance & Estate Specialist (coverage, and beneficiaries changed when the court allows, since changes can be restricted while the case is open and a divorce does not change an ERISA plan's form).

## A new child

- Seats: The Insurance & Estate Specialist (the health plan the child joins, life and disability needs, a will naming a guardian, beneficiaries and contingents through a trust or a custodian, never the child by name), the Investment & Retirement Analyst (adding the child at work inside the plan's window, the dependent care FSA, and education savings), the Tax Strategist (credits, compared with the dependent care FSA, and the child's Social Security number), the Cash Flow Analyst (childcare in the budget).
- A government-seeded child account, if the IRS offers one for the child's birth year, is an IRA-type account, not education savings: The Tax Strategist confirms eligibility and dates its election at IRS.gov, and the Investment & Retirement Analyst weighs it separately.
- Time-critical: the health plan's special enrollment window, often 30 days at work; any election for a government-seeded child account.

## A death in the family, or an inheritance

- Pause: no irreversible decision for weeks to months unless a deadline forces it.
- Time-critical: a qualified disclaimer, if any heir may want one, within nine months of the death and before accepting the asset (with the estate attorney); a surviving spouse's portability election, made on an estate tax return filed on time even when no tax is due; the owner's required distribution for the year of death, when the owner had begun them and had not taken it; life insurance, pension and SSA survivor claims.
- Seats: The Insurance & Estate Specialist (life insurance and beneficiary claims; an estate attorney for probate), the Tax Strategist (inherited retirement accounts: most heirs other than a spouse must empty one by the end of the tenth year after the death and, when the owner had already begun required distributions, take one every year in between; a minor child of the owner, a disabled or chronically ill heir, or one not more than ten years younger follows other rules, fetched at IRS.gov; the step-up in basis on inherited taxable assets, which retirement accounts do not get; estate and inheritance tax thresholds, federal and state), the Investment & Retirement Analyst (placing the inheritance, and survivor benefits from the SSA when a spouse dies), the Cash Flow Analyst (the household budget; the estate, not the heirs, generally pays the deceased's debts: check before paying any from personal funds).
- Never cash out an inherited retirement account to park the money: it moves by direct trustee-to-trustee transfer into an inherited account first.
- Order several certified death certificates; notify the SSA.

## A windfall (a bonus, an equity sale, a settlement, a large gift)

- New cash goes to insured cash or Treasury bills with the tax set aside (an equity sale's tax with the Tax Strategist), spread across banks above the deposit insurance limit; wait, then spend it by the foundations order in `doctrine.md` (the lead, with the Cash Flow Analyst and the Investment & Retirement Analyst). Inherited securities stay as they are until the plan is made, and an inherited retirement account is never cashed out to park it.
- A bonus or a sale in a year the household buys Marketplace coverage is checked against the premium tax credit first.

## Moving to another state

- Seats: The Tax Strategist (residency and domicile, part-year returns, the new state's tax), the Cash Flow Analyst (housing, and the move in the budget), the Insurance & Estate Specialist (insurance re-quoted for the new state, and estate documents checked against its law).
- File the change of address with the IRS and update every account.

## Buying or selling a home

- Seats: The Cash Flow Analyst leads the purchase (rent or buy, affordability, the mortgage, the reserve after closing), then the Insurance & Estate Specialist (homeowners and title coverage), the Tax Strategist (the home-sale exclusion when selling).

## Going self-employed or starting a company

- Seats: The Tax Strategist (quarterly estimated payments, self-employment tax, the premium tax credit's income limits when the household buys Marketplace coverage, and any unvested equity and option windows left at the old employer), the Cash Flow Analyst (irregular income and a nine-to-twelve-month reserve), the Insurance & Estate Specialist (health coverage priced net of the premium tax credit, disability and liability coverage), the Investment & Retirement Analyst (a solo 401(k) or SEP IRA, and the match forfeited at the old employer).
- The venture itself is `board`'s, staffed by AR's Finance & Accounting department; keep the business and personal books separate from day one.

## Approaching retirement

- Seats: The Investment & Retirement Analyst (the drawdown plan, Social Security timing, a pension's lump-sum-or-annuity choice), the Tax Strategist (conversions in low-income years, checked against the Medicare income surcharge and, before 65 on a Marketplace plan, the premium tax credit's income limits; required distributions at the age the current rules set), the Insurance & Estate Specialist (Medicare enrollment with the state's free SHIP counseling, and long-term care), the Cash Flow Analyst (the retirement budget).
- Time-critical: Medicare's initial enrollment period, the seven months around the 65th birthday, since enrolling late can carry lifelong penalties; Part B delayed only while group coverage from current employment pays first, which the plan confirms, because at an employer with fewer than 20 employees Medicare usually pays first, so enroll in Part B when first eligible; never delayed on COBRA or a retiree plan; the Medigap open enrollment window; HSA contributions stopped six months before applying for Medicare or Social Security after 65, because Part A is backdated.

## A serious illness, an injury or a disability

- Seats: The Insurance & Estate Specialist (disability and health claims), the Investment & Retirement Analyst (employer leave and benefits, and Social Security disability, which pays only after a waiting period, so apply early), the Cash Flow Analyst (an essentials budget, means-tested benefits if income falls, and hardship programs before any payment is missed); confirm the powers of attorney and the health care directive are in place.

## Identity theft or fraud

- Seats: The Cash Flow Analyst (freeze all three bureaus, fraud alerts, the recovery plan at IdentityTheft.gov, disputes for fraudulent accounts), the Tax Strategist (an IRS Identity Protection PIN, and the IRS identity theft process if a false return was filed).
- Change compromised passwords; anything kept records only that it was done and where credentials live.

## Caring for an aging parent

- Seats: The Insurance & Estate Specialist (long-term care and an elder law attorney), the Tax Strategist (dependent status and credits), the Cash Flow Analyst (the care costs in the budget).
