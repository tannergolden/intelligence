# Voice - loading your user's voice, honestly

Voice fidelity is the team's defining craft rule, ranked beneath clarity. It is what makes a piece your user's own rather than generic. But it is honest: the skill claims your user's voice **only when it actually has it**, and often it does not.

## Load the voice first

Before drafting anything, load your user's voice from the best source there is, in this order:

1. **A voice profile the repository keeps.** Where the repository's instructions say it is a private one made to hold your user's personal context and name where the profile lives (a working-style, personality, or preferences note, or a voice guide), read it. The facet that most directly shapes a draft covers how to be addressed, default length, tone, do's and don'ts, and terminology.
2. **Samples of their own writing.** Text your user wrote, in the conversation or the repository (a sent email they paste, their own notes or posts), read for the concrete features below.
3. **Neither:** no voice. Use the neutral defaults below and say so.

The lead loads this at the start of every piece and carries it into every dispatch brief; no agent invents a voice the source already settles. Never write the loaded voice, or anything else about your user, into a repository unless its instructions say it is a private one made to hold it.

## When there is no real voice: neutral defaults, declared

> [!WARNING]
> **An empty profile is not a voice.**
> A profile can exist and still be an **empty scaffold**: placeholder text like "terse / balanced / thorough" or "(e.g. a first name, a title)", not real answers. Treat it exactly as no profile.

**When no real voice is loaded, editorial does NOT claim your user's voice.** Instead it:

1. **Writes in neutral best-practice defaults** (below), not in a guessed voice.
2. **Says so**, in one plain line: "I have no sample of your voice yet, so this is in a neutral default voice, not yours."
3. **Offers to capture the real voice**, two ways: have your user **paste two or three real samples** (a sent email, a note they wrote) to draft from directly for this piece, or, where the repository keeps a profile, call the **`identify` skill** to fill it through a structured interview.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Never synthesize a voice from placeholder scaffolding.**
> A line that reads "terse / balanced / thorough" is a menu of options, not a setting. Reading it as "your user is terse" is inventing a voice from a form field. Do not do it. An empty facet means no voice, not a default-to-the-first-word voice.

**Voice fidelity activates once a real voice is loaded.** From that point the loaded features drive the draft and the Line Editor checks conformance against them. Until then, neutral defaults plus the honest disclaimer.

## Neutral best-practice defaults (used until a real voice is loaded)

- **Structure:** bottom-line-up-front; scannable; one idea per paragraph; short paragraphs.
- **Register:** consultative (Joos's middle rung): professional, warm, not stiff, not chatty. See `email.md`.
- **Email sign-off:** "Best regards," for a first or formal contact; "Best," or "Thanks," once a thread is warm.
- **Tone:** direct, specific, plain. No slop (`slop-rubric.md`), no em dash.
- **Length:** match the input and the medium; a short ask gets a short answer.

These are defaults, not a claimed voice; they are what a careful writer does absent any personal signal.

## A voice profile wants concrete features, never adjectives

When a voice is captured (from samples, or by `identify` where the repository keeps a profile), a usable voice profile is built from explicit, checkable features, not from words like "professional" or "friendly" that mean nothing operational:

- **Samples per format.** Real sent text for each format that matters (email, message, doc, post). Samples are the ground truth; everything below is distilled from them.
- **Structural DNA.** Typical sentence length and paragraph length; opener and closer habits; the actual **greeting and sign-off** used, per relationship.
- **Semantic DNA.** **Signature phrases** your user reaches for, and **banned words** they never use (their personal slop list, on top of standards' Plain Words list).
- **Tone on named scales.** Position on concrete axes (formal <-> casual, warm <-> reserved, terse <-> expansive, plain <-> playful), not a single adjective.
- **Register baseline plus per-relationship overrides.** The default register, and how it shifts for named relationships (a client vs a close colleague vs a vendor).

> [!TIP]
> **Capture features and samples, never adjectives.**
> "Friendly and professional" is not a voice; it cannot be checked against a draft. "Opens with the person's first name, one-line context, the ask in the first two sentences; signs 'Best,'; never says 'circle back' or 'touch base'" is a voice. Capture the second kind.

## In the dispatch brief

The lead passes the loaded voice (the concrete features, or the explicit "no voice loaded: neutral defaults, do not invent a voice" note) into every Writer and Line Editor brief, so the worker drafts to the real signal or the honest default, never to a guess. The Line Editor checks voice conformance only when there is a real voice to check against; with no real voice loaded, it checks clarity and slop and notes that voice conformance is not yet checkable.
