# Voice - loading the user's voice, honestly

Voice fidelity is the team's defining craft rule, ranked beneath clarity. It is what makes a piece the user's own rather than generic. But it is honest: the skill claims the user's voice **only when it actually has it**, and often it does not.

## Load the voice first

Before drafting anything, load the user's voice from the best source there is, in this order:

1. **A voice profile the repository keeps.** Where the repository's instructions say it is a private one made to hold the user's personal context and name where the profile lives (a working-style, personality, or preferences note, or a voice guide), read it. The facet that most directly shapes a draft covers how to be addressed, default length, tone, do's and don'ts, and terminology.
2. **Samples of their own writing.** Text the user wrote, in the conversation or the repository (a sent email they paste, their own notes or posts), read for the concrete features below.
3. **Neither:** no voice. Use the neutral defaults below and say so.

The lead loads this at the start of every piece and carries it into every dispatch brief; no agent invents a voice the source already settles. Never write the loaded voice, or anything else about the user, into a repository unless its instructions say it is a private one made to hold it.

## When there is no real voice: neutral defaults, declared

> [!WARNING]
> **An empty profile is not a voice.**
> A profile can exist and still be an **empty scaffold**: placeholder text like "terse / balanced / thorough" or "(e.g. a first name, a title)", not real answers. Treat it exactly as no profile.

**When no real voice is loaded, the team does NOT claim the user's voice.** Instead it:

1. **Writes in neutral best-practice defaults** (below), not in a guessed voice.
2. **Says so**, in one plain line: "I have no sample of your voice yet, so this is in a neutral default voice, not yours."
3. **Offers to capture the real voice**, two ways: have the user **paste two or three real samples** (a sent email, a note they wrote) to draft from directly for this piece, or, where the repository keeps a profile, run the **human-resources skill's profile interview** to fill it through a structured interview.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Never synthesize a voice from placeholder scaffolding.**
> A line that reads "terse / balanced / thorough" is a menu of options, not a setting. Reading it as "the user is terse" is inventing a voice from a form field. Do not do it. An empty facet means no voice, not a default-to-the-first-word voice.

**Voice fidelity activates once a real voice is loaded.** From that point the loaded features drive the draft and the Editor checks conformance against them. Until then, neutral defaults plus the honest disclaimer.

## Neutral best-practice defaults (used until a real voice is loaded)

- **Structure:** bottom-line-up-front; scannable; one idea per paragraph; short paragraphs.
- **Register:** consultative (Joos's middle rung): professional, warm, not stiff, not chatty. See `email.md`.
- **Email sign-off:** "Best regards," for a first or formal contact; "Best," or "Thanks," once a thread is warm.
- **Tone:** direct, specific, plain. No slop (`slop-rubric.md`), no em dash.
- **Length:** match the input and the medium; a short ask gets a short answer.

These are defaults, not a claimed voice; they are what a careful writer does absent any personal signal.

## A voice profile wants concrete features, never adjectives

When a voice is captured (from samples, or by the human-resources skill's profile interview where the repository keeps a profile), a usable voice profile is built from explicit, checkable features, not from words like "professional" or "friendly" that mean nothing operational:

- **Samples per format.** Real sent text for each format that matters (email, message, doc, post). Samples are the ground truth; everything below is distilled from them.
- **Structural DNA.** Typical sentence length and paragraph length; opener and closer habits; the actual **greeting and sign-off** used, per relationship.
- **Semantic DNA.** **Signature phrases** the user reaches for, and **banned words** they never use (their personal slop list, on top of standards' Plain Words list).
- **Tone on named scales.** Position on concrete axes (formal <-> casual, warm <-> reserved, terse <-> expansive, plain <-> playful), not a single adjective.
- **Register baseline plus per-relationship overrides.** The default register, and how it shifts for named relationships (a client vs a close colleague vs a vendor).

> [!TIP]
> **Capture features and samples, never adjectives.**
> "Friendly and professional" is not a voice; it cannot be checked against a draft. "Opens with the person's first name, one-line context, the ask in the first two sentences; signs 'Best,'; never says 'circle back' or 'touch base'" is a voice. Capture the second kind.

## In the dispatch brief

The lead passes the loaded voice (the concrete features, or the explicit "no voice loaded: neutral defaults, do not invent a voice" note) into every Writer and Editor brief, so the worker drafts to the real signal or the honest default, never to a guess. The Editor checks voice conformance only when there is a real voice to check against; with no real voice loaded, it checks clarity and slop and notes that voice conformance is not yet checkable.

## When the piece speaks for a company

A piece the company says in its own name (a release, a statement, an announcement, a post on its channels, a letter to every customer) carries the company's one voice, not the user's personal one: the messaging house's umbrella message and pillars, its words to use and to avoid, and its approved spokesperson lines (`templates/message-house.md`), which sit inside the positioning the sales-and-marketing skill owns. The lead passes that voice into the brief in place of the user's; with no messaging house yet, it passes the positioning and the confirmed facts and says the company voice is not yet set. When the user speaks for themselves in public (a personal statement, a bio, a post), the voice is theirs, loaded as above.
