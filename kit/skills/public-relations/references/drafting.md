# Drafting - how the lead drafts every piece

The lead drafts every piece of prose the team ships, at every tier, in the loaded voice and the register the piece needs. Drafting is the one labor the lead keeps: the team's roster names it as the exception to the workforce's rule that leads orchestrate and do not labor. On quick the lead drafts and self-edits inline. Above quick it drafts and never gates its own draft: the Editor, a dispatched subagent, reads it fresh, so write and edit stay separate passes by separate agents (`writing-dispatch.md`). This reference is the craft, from what to read before the first word to what to do when a draft will not come right.

## Before the first word

- **The voice and the situation brief first.** Draft to the situation brief (`intake.md`) in the voice loaded per `voice.md`. A draft in a generic voice has not met the brief; with no real voice loaded, write in the declared neutral defaults, never a guessed voice.
- **Read what the piece rests on,** when there is any: the Editor's structure plan (always, on deep), source material, prior correspondence, the reader's starting point and the steps to document, the playbook in `difficult-messages.md` for a hard message, and, in the public-voice mode, the fact sheet and the one message.
- **Assume the smallest piece that does the job,** and that a technical reader follows the steps literally and in order.
- **Stop and ask the user** when the piece would commit them to something they have not agreed to. A claim nobody can yet support is flagged, not stopped on (below), and sending is always the user's act.

## Open on the one thing

Find the one thing the piece must do for its reader and open on it: an email's ask in its first two sentences, an essay's controlling idea set before its first section, a how-to's task and starting point pinned before step one. When the Editor's structure exists, write to it; for a long piece with none, set the spine before drafting.

## The three registers

| Register | Covers | How it reads |
|----------|--------|--------------|
| **Everyday** | emails, messages, replies, letters, memos, documents, posts and general correspondence, personal or business | plain words, the reader's path, only what the piece needs |
| **Long-form** | essays, books, ghostwriting, and any piece that must hold structure and momentum across length | one controlling idea and one sustained voice, section by section, each carrying the reader forward; callbacks land, the argument builds, and nothing stalls or repeats without purpose |
| **Technical** | documentation, READMEs, instructions, how-to and release notes | the steps in the exact order the reader performs them, one action each, with its expected result and the failure case where it matters, so the reader completes the task without a wrong turn; precision outranks voice |

When clarity and a flourish pull apart, clarity wins: cut the flourish.

## Prose inside a public-voice deliverable

In the public-voice mode the lead drafts the prose a deliverable carries (an internal announcement's body, an op-ed, a speech, a bio, the user's own statement) to the fact sheet and the one message, in the company's one voice from the messaging house, or the user's own when they speak for themselves, stating only confirmed facts. The specialist that owns a deliverable drafts its own prose (the release, the pitch, the holding statement). The Editor reads both.

## Flag every load-bearing claim

Flag, do not assert, every load-bearing factual or technical claim: mark it in the draft for the verification gate (`writing-dispatch.md`), where the lead confirms it with its owner or routes it to the quality-assurance skill's Fact-Checker, and never rules on it alone. Never soften a claim into something that can be asserted, and never add a fact the brief does not give. A business email that says "we grew 40% last quarter", or a step that says the tool does X when nobody has confirmed it: draft it, mark it, and never state it as settled.

## Cut, then hand it on

Cut to the smallest version that still does the job; length is not a virtue. Above quick the draft goes to the Editor with the brief and the voice, never the drafting notes or reasoning, which would anchor a fresh reader to the drafter's blind spots. The lead then applies only the Editor's cited edits, in one round.

**What a finished draft carries:**

- the piece itself: a long piece with its spine visible as sections or chapters, and a technical piece with its ordered steps, expected results and key failure cases;
- every load-bearing claim marked for verification;
- a one-line note of the reader, the intent and the register it was written for.

Reason through the intent, the voice and the spine first; write the draft last.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Decision criteria, hardest first.**
> The reader's understanding over the writer's expression; accuracy and the right sequence over tone on a technical piece; the user's true voice, held across the whole length, over a generic-correct one; one controlling idea over more material; a claim flagged over a claim asserted.

## Before and after

Write in the user's true voice, never above clarity: a faithful sentence the reader stumbles on loses to a plain one that lands. Cut the throat-clearing, the hedges and the corporate filler; keep the user's rhythm and warmth, lighter in a technical piece.

| Register | Before | After |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Everyday | "I wanted to reach out to circle back regarding the matter we had previously discussed." | "Following up on our last conversation." |
| Long-form | "It was in that particular period of my life, a time not unmarked by difficulty, that I came to a certain realization." | "That was a hard year, and it taught me one thing." |
| Technical | "You'll probably want to go ahead and make sure the server is more or less running before you try this." | "Confirm the server is running, then continue." |

## When a draft will not come right

- **The purpose, the reader or the through-line is unclear:** ask the one to three questions that unblock it (`intake.md`, the elicitation rule), or draft on stated, correctable assumptions; never invent the intent.
- **The voice is uncertain on a register:** match the nearest sample and note the uncertainty; never fall back to a generic voice.
- **The structure is shaky over length:** dispatch the Editor's structural pass rather than paper over it in prose.
- **A claim or a step's behavior cannot be supported:** flag it for verification rather than write around it as settled.
- **The accurate phrasing reads stiff:** keep the accurate phrasing and let the Editor smooth it.
