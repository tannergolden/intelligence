# Deliverables - what the Public Relations team produces, and where it lands

The department's producing map: each deliverable, the seat that produces it, what triggers it, the completion evidence checked before it lands, and where it goes. It is the Public Relations slice of the company's deliverable catalog, made universal, and it covers both modes: the write mode's pieces of prose, for the user's own life or a company they run (first, below), and the public-voice mode's deliverables. "The venture's Public Relations records" means wherever the repository's instructions keep them (a venture folder, a repository of the company's own); with no such instruction, the conversation. Every deliverable has one producing seat and one accountable lead, the ★ Chief Communications Officer.

## How to read an entry

- **Seat** produces it. The lead frames the brief and assembles the result, and never verifies what the team made.
- **Trigger** is what calls for it.
- **Completion evidence** is the Definition of Done checked before the skill writes it or presents it as ready: by the Editor for a piece inside the team, and by the quality-assurance skill's ★ Head of Quality for anything consequential going outside. A producer never verifies its own work, and a brief without completion evidence goes back to the lead to define done first.
- **Lands in** names where it goes: for a piece of prose, its home (below); for a public-voice deliverable, which of the venture's Public Relations records holds it: the **messaging record** (the messaging house and spokesperson lines), the **press record** (communications plans, the press kit, drafts and released statements, and the public log of what the company has said), or the **crisis record** (the standing crisis plan, and each live response with its update log).
- **Needs** names another skill's input, produced or flagged before the deliverable is built on it.
- **Gated** names the acts that wait for the user's explicit confirmation. Drafting is never gated; releasing always is.

## The write mode: a piece of prose

Most runs produce one piece of prose, for the user's own life or for a company they run: an email, a message or a reply, a letter, a memo, a document, a post, an essay or a book chapter, a README, a docs page, release notes or a how-to. Its home is the file under edit, where the repository's instructions put that kind of file, or where the user names; a leaving piece goes to the user in the conversation, or to a file they choose, and is never filed into the repository.

- **Seat:** the ★ Chief Communications Officer drafts at every tier (`drafting.md`), and self-edits on quick; above quick the Editor reads it fresh, structure first, then the line, and the lead never gates its own draft.
- **Trigger:** a request to draft, write, edit, rewrite, reply, proofread, tighten or format, or prose being written or edited in a repository (`SKILL.md`, Boundaries, scopes it).
- **Completion evidence:** the situation read done and, above quick, the situation brief written (the goal, the reader and the relationship, the one key message, the sentiment and tone call); the voice loaded, or the neutral default declared; the Editor's pass clean, with no structural flaw open and no hard-gate violation (DOC-002, DOC-003); every load-bearing claim verified with its owner or the quality-assurance skill's Fact-Checker; formatted for its destination, with the repository's named check passing for a filed piece; the author's length and register kept unless the user asked to change them; a deep-tier piece verified by the quality-assurance skill.
- **Lands in:** its home, as above; an edit in place is shown as a diff or a redline before it lands.
- **Needs:** the facts it asserts, from their owners; for a company's piece, the messaging house and the positioning, so it says what the company says.
- **Gated:** sending, posting or publishing it, the user's personal email included; drafting it never is.

Two pieces have a borrowed caller. The human-resources skill's profile interview borrows the Editor to compose its proposal batch from the interview ledger alone, in the user's own voice, every inference marked, in the shape of that skill's `templates/proposal-batch.md` and following its `references/provenance.md`; the Editor composes the batch and never reviews it, it persists nothing, and that skill has the batch reviewed blind by another seat and keeps only what the user approves. And another skill may hand this one prose to polish on its own brief (copy for the sales-and-marketing skill, a reply for the customer-experience skill, a research report for the research-and-development skill): the Editor polishes it with its meaning fixed, and the calling skill owns what it claims.

For the public-voice mode, stage scales the set. A company before launch needs the messaging house, a boilerplate and a communications plan for each announcement; a scaling company adds the press kit, the standing crisis plan and its media relationships; a mature company keeps the whole set current and reviews the crisis plan on a cadence. Produce the smallest set the stage needs, never the full catalog by reflex. For the user in public, the set is smaller still: a current bio, the lines they hold to on the topics they are asked about, and a ready holding line when an issue is live, kept only in a private repository made to hold the user's personal context, and otherwise returned in the conversation.

## Standing assets

- **The messaging house and spokesperson lines** (messaging record): the umbrella message, the pillars and their proof points, the approved lines by topic, and the lines not to cross.
- **The press kit and the public log** (press record): the boilerplate, the fact sheet, current releases, approved bios and images, and a dated log of every statement the company has released, with its channel and spokesperson. The log is what makes "consistent with the record" checkable.
- **The crisis plan** (crisis record): the severity levels and their triggers, who decides and who speaks at each, the escalation path, and pre-cleared holding statements for the likely scenarios.

## Recurring outputs

### Messaging house and spokesperson lines

- **Seat:** ★ Chief Communications Officer.
- **Trigger:** the company's first public voice; a launch, a raise or a new segment; a repositioning; a new spokesperson; a crisis that needs lines.
- **Completion evidence:** an umbrella message a stranger can repeat in one sentence; at most three pillars, each with proof points that trace to confirmed facts; lines for the topics the audiences will raise, each naming who may say it; the lines not to cross; consistent with the positioning on record and with the public log, or a deliberate update that says what changed.
- **Lands in:** the messaging record.
- **Needs:** the positioning from the sales-and-marketing skill, whose Marketing Manager owns it: the messaging house sits inside it and never redefines it; any metric it cites, defined by the business-intelligence skill.
- **Gated:** nothing; it is internal until a statement uses it.

### Communications plan

- **Seat:** ★ Chief Communications Officer.
- **Trigger:** a launch, a milestone, a raise, a leadership change, a reorganization, or anything else the outside or the whole company will see.
- **Completion evidence:** the stakeholder map in order (who hears it, what they must take away, from whom, through which channel, when), each audience's message tied to the messaging house; the assets with their seats and status; the timing and any embargo; employees hear before or with the public, never after; every send listed for the user's confirmation.
- **Lands in:** the press record.
- **Needs:** the sales-and-marketing skill's launch plan when it is a launch (the campaign is theirs; the announcement to the press, the public and the company is this team's); the human-resources skill for what a change means for employees; the administration skill's decision when the matter is company-level.
- **Gated:** every send in it.

### Press release and press kit

- **Seat:** Media Relations Manager drafts; the Chief Communications Officer clears.
- **Trigger:** a launch, a milestone, a raise, a partnership, a key hire, or any news worth announcing.
- **Completion evidence:** the news in the headline and the first paragraph (who, what, when, where, why); every fact, figure and name traced to the fact sheet and confirmed; every quote approved by the person quoted, and a partner's or customer's in writing; a current boilerplate and a media contact the user named; the embargo line where there is one; the status marker; legal clearance recorded where a trigger applied.
- **Lands in:** the press record.
- **Needs:** financial figures from the finance-and-accounting skill, metrics from the business-intelligence skill, technical claims about the product confirmed by the research-and-development skill's ★ Head of Research & Development, a partner's approval of anything said about the partner, and a fact about the world checked against its source by the quality-assurance skill's Fact-Checker.
- **Gated:** publishing it, sending it to any outlet or wire service, and sharing it under embargo.

### Media pitch

- **Seat:** Media Relations Manager.
- **Trigger:** a story worth earned coverage, or a reporter the company wants a relationship with.
- **Completion evidence:** the reporter and the outlet matched to the beat, with their recent coverage cited and dated; the hook and why now, in one sentence; the pitch short and on message; any terms offered (an exclusive, an embargo) stated; nothing in it unconfirmed.
- **Lands in:** the press record.
- **Gated:** sending it, and any terms it offers.

### Briefing and interview prep

- **Seat:** Media Relations Manager, with the lead's lines and, on a sensitive subject, the Crisis & Issues Communications Manager's reactive lines.
- **Trigger:** an interview, a press briefing, a podcast, a panel, or a call with a reporter.
- **Completion evidence:** the reporter, the outlet, their recent coverage and likely angle; the format and the ground rules (on the record unless agreed otherwise, in advance and in writing); the three messages to land; the likely and the hard questions, each with an answer that bridges back to a message; the lines not to cross; the confirmed facts and figures to have at hand.
- **Lands in:** the press record.
- **Gated:** agreeing ground rules or an embargo with the reporter; the interview itself is the spokesperson's own act.

### Response to a press inquiry

- **Seat:** Media Relations Manager for a routine inquiry; the Chief Communications Officer for a sensitive one or a correction request; the Crisis & Issues Communications Manager when it touches a live issue.
- **Trigger:** a reporter's question, with its deadline.
- **Completion evidence:** the question as asked, the outlet and the deadline; the answer or the holding line, on message, every fact confirmed; whether to answer on the record, on background or not at all, with the reason.
- **Lands in:** the press record.
- **Gated:** sending any answer.

### Crisis plan (standing)

- **Seat:** Crisis & Issues Communications Manager, under the Chief Communications Officer.
- **Trigger:** a risk the company can name in advance (a launch into a regulated area, a security exposure, a product safety risk, a contentious change) or a stage change; reviewed on a cadence and after every crisis.
- **Completion evidence:** the severity levels with their triggers; who decides and who speaks at each level; the escalation path, with the legal-and-compliance skill and the administration skill named at the level they join; pre-cleared holding statements for the likely scenarios; the stakeholder sequence; how the company will learn early that an issue is spreading.
- **Lands in:** the crisis record.
- **Needs:** the legal-and-compliance skill to pre-clear the holding statements; the information-technology skill's incident response for a technical or security failure.
- **Gated:** nothing until it is used.

### Crisis response and holding statements (live)

- **Seat:** Crisis & Issues Communications Manager; the Chief Communications Officer clears.
- **Trigger:** a live issue or incident.
- **Completion evidence:** the severity and trajectory call; the fact line (confirmed, alleged, unknown, each with the owner who confirms it); a holding statement that acknowledges, shows care, says what the company is doing, commits to the next update by a named time, and asserts nothing unconfirmed; the stakeholder sequence (who hears first, from whom, through which channel); the reactive questions and answers; every item flagged to the legal-and-compliance skill or the lead; the update log.
- **Lands in:** the crisis record; once it is closed, its review updates the standing crisis plan.
- **Needs:** the incident's facts from the skill that owns them (the information-technology skill for an outage or a breach, the quality-assurance skill for a defect, the customer-experience skill for who is affected, the human-resources skill for a matter about an employee); the legal-and-compliance skill's clearance before a release; the administration skill's call when the company may not speak at all.
- **Gated:** every release and every send in the sequence.

### Internal announcement

- **Seat:** ★ Chief Communications Officer, who decides its message and drafts its prose; the Editor reads it fresh.
- **Trigger:** a change employees should hear from the company first: a reorganization, a leadership change, an incident, a launch, a raise.
- **Completion evidence:** the news, why, what it means for the people reading it, and whom to ask; consistent with the external message and released before or with it; the facts about roles, pay or policy confirmed by the human-resources skill; written as if it will be read outside the company, because it will be.
- **Lands in:** the press record.
- **Needs:** the human-resources skill for anything about roles, pay, policy or employment.
- **Gated:** sending it; an all-staff message cannot be unsent.

## The producing order

A deliverable is never built on a missing input. Before producing one, confirm its upstream input exists and is current; if it does not, produce it or flag it first, and the verifier checks that the inputs were real, not assumed.

- **The voice:** positioning (the sales-and-marketing skill) -> the messaging house -> the communications plan -> the press release, pitches and briefings -> the interview -> the public log.
- **A statement:** facts confirmed by their owners -> the draft -> legal clearance when a trigger applies -> verification -> the user's release -> the public log.
- **A crisis:** the issue seen -> severity and trajectory -> the fact line -> the holding statement -> the stakeholder sequence -> the updates, each on time -> the review that updates the standing crisis plan.

## The produce loop

The lead frames the deliverable and its completion evidence; the seat that owns it produces it and returns it with that evidence, persisting nothing; the lead assembles it and checks it against the audience, the one message and the record, which is a first-line check, not the verification; the Editor polishes it when the prose would gain from it, with the message and the facts fixed; the quality-assurance skill's ★ Head of Quality verifies it, blind to how it was made; the skill writes the draft or the record behind its write-gate; and only the user releases anything outside the company. In an administration run, the administration skill convenes this lead and persists the run's results.
