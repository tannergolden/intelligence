# Dispatch - how the tiers map onto the workforce

How `administration` spends the workforce (`workforce.md`), scaled to the decision. Read before choosing a tier. The workforce is the chair (the ★ Chief Executive Officer) plus the rest of the Administration team (the Corporate Secretary, the Chief Operating Officer, and the Chief of Staff), all in this skill's `agents/`, and the department leads, each in its own skill's `agents/`: the finance-and-accounting skill's ★ Chief Financial Officer, the legal-and-compliance skill's ★ General Counsel, the sales-and-marketing skill's ★ Chief Revenue Officer, the operations-and-logistics skill's ★ Head of Operations, the human-resources skill's ★ Chief Human Resources Officer, the information-technology skill's ★ Chief Information Officer, the research-and-development skill's ★ Head of Research & Development, the business-intelligence skill's ★ Chief Data Officer, the quality-assurance skill's ★ Head of Quality, the customer-experience skill's ★ Chief Customer Officer, and the public-relations skill's ★ Chief Communications Officer. On a product question the chair draws on the research-and-development skill for the evidence about users and markets, the options, and technical direction and feasibility. Every dispatch is a self-contained brief; the subagent sees none of the conversation; department leads run in parallel and never see each other's views while forming their own; the chair alone holds the question, synthesizes, and names the owner.

## Tier decision table

| If the decision is | Tier | Convene |
|---|---|---|
| small, low-stakes, or inside one department | **quick** | chair plus the 1 to 2 relevant department leads; no formal dissent pass; call it. (A department's own work with no company or life call in it is that department skill's run, not administration's.) |
| a real cross-department call, or a personal decision (a move, a job offer, a big purchase) | **standard** | chair (who lays out the MECE options in framing) plus the relevant department leads (independent views), one dissent role (the quality-assurance skill's Devil's Advocate, or a pre-mortem), and the Corporate Secretary (record); on a personal decision, the Founder as the decider |
| high-stakes, irreversible, contested, or cross-venture | **deep / board-grade** | every relevant department, a dissent battery (devil's advocacy, pre-mortem, red team), a scoring or dialectical pass, a scenario test, the COO (execution plan, or the portfolio mix on a cross-venture call), and the Chief of Staff (relay to every department the call binds); escalate to the Founder as decider |

When two rows fit, take the higher. Announce the tier before dispatching; it is a cost decision made with the owner's tokens. One decision per run.

## How each seat is briefed

- **The chair (CEO).** Frames the question (SCQA), lays out a MECE set of 2 to 3 genuine options and their second-order effects in framing, flags each as reversible or irreversible and recommends (the Founder decides major calls and every personal one), names the relevant lenses and the criteria, runs the synthesis, and states a view last. On a product decision the chair is also the decider of what to build, on the evidence. Not dispatched as a worker; the chair is the run.
- **Department leads (the lenses).** Each is dispatched from its own skill's `agents/` with the same framed question, the same criteria, and the output contract: an independent written view (recommendation, assumptions, tradeoffs). Dispatched in parallel, blind to each other. Convene only the lenses the decision touches (`lenses.md`); on a personal decision, the departments whose personal scope it touches, each briefed on the Founder's own goals and constraints.
- **Dissent role.** The quality-assurance skill's Devil's Advocate, read from that skill's `agents/` and briefed to argue against the front-runner, or to imagine the decision has failed and work back (pre-mortem). On deep, a red team and a dialectical counter-plan are separate briefs.
- **Corporate Secretary.** Briefed to record the decision memo (`memo.md`), including the minority view by role.
- **Chief Operating Officer.** The honest broker; briefed on deep runs to pressure-test the synthesis for the chair before the call hardens, and to turn the decided call into an execution plan (the next steps, the owners, the dates). When ventures or projects compete for the same capital, time or attention, the Founder's personal ones included, briefed to score and balance the portfolio and recommend the mix (`strategy-toolkit.md`, Group E).
- **Chief of Staff.** Briefed once the call is made to carry it, restated self-contained, to the lead of every department it binds, and to turn it into commitments, each with an owner, a date, and a done-test; when the follow-through needs seats from several departments, it forms a temporary working group of only those seats and closes it when the job is done.

## The one rule that does not bend

The chair holds the question and the synthesis. Department leads form views in isolation and advise; no lead grades its own view, and no lead synthesizes. The decider is one seat (the CEO, or the Founder on major calls and on every personal one), and the decision names one accountable owner. This is the standard in operating form: every angle heard, one owner accountable.
