# Memo - the decision record

The structure of the decide mode's output: a decision memo that leads with the recommendation and preserves the dissent, for a company call or a life choice alike. Read before recording. The scaffold is `templates/decision-memo.md`. Delivered in chat by default; filed on request (below).

## Answer first (the conventions)

Authoritative memo conventions converge on one rule: put the answer at the top.

- **BLUF, bottom line up front.** Put the conclusion and the recommendation in the opening, then the support. Codified in US Army Regulation 25-50; the rule is to put the main point at the beginning and use the active voice, so busy readers decide faster (the BLUF convention).
- **SCQA (Minto).** Frame the opening as Situation, Complication, Question, Answer, then group the supporting arguments (ideally three), each Mutually Exclusive and Collectively Exhaustive (Minto, the Pyramid Principle).
- **Amazon's narrative memo.** Prose, not slides: complete sentences and paragraphs force a deeper clarity than bullet points. The memo is read in silence at the top of the meeting before any discussion. For a new initiative, the PR/FAQ works backward from the customer (a mock press release plus the external and internal FAQs that anticipate the finance, legal, and operations objections); the aim is truth-seeking, not selling.
- **The board paper.** Each item is titled for noting, for discussion, or for decision; the recommendation is a page or less and names the options considered and the criteria used to choose; an options table scores each option on the criteria; the risk section rates each major risk's likelihood and impact with mitigations (the board-paper convention).

## The recommended structure

The memo the skill produces, in order:

1. **Decision requested and recommendation (BLUF).** One line on the decision; one to two on the recommendation, with the single accountable owner named. The answer, first.
2. **Situation and complication (SCQA).** The shared context, what changed, and why the call is live now.
3. **The question.** The exact choice being put, at the right altitude, with the decision criteria.
4. **Options considered.** The 2 to 3 genuine options, MECE, scored against the criteria (a table). Include the option not taken and why.
5. **Each department's view and the tradeoffs.** The independent views of the convened departments (finance and accounting, legal and compliance, marketing, operations and logistics, and the rest), each in a line or two, with the tradeoffs each surfaced. This is the multi-perspective record.
6. **Risks and pre-mortem.** The major risks rated by likelihood and impact, with mitigations, plus the pre-mortem causes (the ways this fails a year out).
7. **Decision and owner.** The call, the decider, and the one accountable owner. Note where it escalated to the Founder; on a personal call the Founder is the decider, named as such.
8. **Next steps.** The actions, each with an owner and a date.
9. **Minority view.** The dissent, captured by role and reason (below). Present even when the call is confident.

## Capturing dissent

A good record channels opposing views rather than silencing them: protocols should channel, not silence, dissent. Board minutes record the motion, the vote method, and the tally, and a director may have their dissent recorded by role, an explicit exception to the usual rule that minutes do not attribute comments (American Bar Association, board-governance guidance; SE4Nonprofits; BoardEffect). Mirror this in the memo with a minority-view line that states the objection and who held it, so the record preserves the strongest counter-argument instead of burying it. A decision that records no dissent on a genuinely hard call is a warning sign, not a clean result: it usually means the dissent was smoothed over, not that it was absent.

## Filing

By default the memo is delivered in chat. On request it is filed (`templates/decision-memo.md`): appended to the venture's `Administration/Decisions.md` when the venture has a record in the repository, or, for a cross-venture call, filed where the repository's instructions keep reports (with any index those instructions name updated in the same edit), or at the path the user names. With no such instruction and no path given, it stays in chat. A decision that must outlive its memo (a standing policy, a committed direction) is offered for promotion into the repository's durable notes, wherever its instructions keep them, not left in the report. A memo on the Founder's own life is personal context: it is filed only where a private repository made to hold it says, by its root and folder `README.md` files, and anywhere else it stays in the conversation.
