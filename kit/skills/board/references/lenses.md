# Lenses - what each function brings

The distinct view each department lead holds on a cross-functional decision, and how to brief each one to get a sharp functional view rather than mush. The chair convenes the lenses the decision actually touches, never all of them by reflex. Each lead is dispatched on the same framed question and the same criteria, and returns an independent written view: its recommendation, its assumptions, and the tradeoffs its function sees.

Cross-functional decisions are better because the structure "puts every needed perspective in the same room, so a decision that would normally take three rounds of email gets made once" (Burrus, "Cross-Functional Team Leadership"). The value comes from the lenses being genuinely different, so protect the difference.

## The functional lenses (the workforce's department leads)

- **Finance and Accounting (CFO): returns, risk, cash.** Owns the ROI and payback case, the capital and cash-flow impact, and the downside scenarios. Note the documented bias: finance leaders tend to speak up "only if their business areas were being discussed," so the chair must explicitly draw the lens onto the whole decision, not just its budget line (McKinsey, "Untangling Your Organization's Decision Making").
- **Legal and Compliance (General Counsel): liability and compliance.** Holds veto-grade input on legal and regulatory thresholds, the Agree role in RAPID (see `synthesis.md`), not a general opinion. Frame the ask as "what would make this impermissible or materially risky," not "what do you think."
- **Marketing (CRO, with Product Marketing Manager producing): customer and brand.** Voice of the customer, brand and reputational fit, the demand response. The lens on whether the market will want and reward the move (brand, demand, customer). Owned by the CRO now that the marketing seats sit in S&R; the brand-voice consistency gate sits with PR (Chief Communications Officer).
- **Sales and Revenue (CRO): the revenue number.** The pipeline, the forecast, the close, and the single revenue number across Marketing, Sales, and Client Experience. A distinct lens from Marketing: where the marketing lens asks whether the market will want the move, the revenue lens asks what it does to bookings, win rate, and the quota-carrying motion. Both feed the venture's Sales & Marketing (S&M)/ folder through distinct files.
- **Operations and Logistics (Head of Operations): feasibility and capacity.** Can the move actually be delivered, at what scale, on what timeline, with what constraints. The lens that turns a good idea into a deliverable one.
- **Human Resources (CHRO): people and culture.** Headcount, the change-management load, morale, and cultural fit. The lens on whether the organization can absorb the move.
- **Information Technology (CIO): systems and security.** Integration cost, data security, technical risk, and what the systems can and cannot support.
- **Business Intelligence (CDO): the evidence.** The data that grounds or undercuts the case; the lens that replaces opinion with measurement where measurement exists.
- **Research and Development (CPO): the product and the future.** Product feasibility, innovation risk, and what the move does to the roadmap.
- **Client Experience (CCO): the lived experience.** What the move does to existing customers, retention, and the support load.
- **Quality Assurance (Head of Quality): the standard.** Whether the move holds the quality bar, and where it risks breaking it.
- **Public Relations (Chief Communications Officer): reputation.** How the move reads externally and internally, and the communications risk.

## Briefing for sharpness, not mush

Give each lead the same decision frame and the same criteria, then ask for an independent written view before any group discussion. Require each function to state its recommendation and its assumptions, since surfacing assumptions is where structured methods add the most value (Schweiger, Sandberg and Ragan, Academy of Management Journal). Amazon's written-memo discipline exists for exactly this reason: "the best ideas come from diverse thinking, but as instinctive people-pleasers, it is hard to voice your opinion if it is different from that of the boss" (the Amazon meeting practice). A view written in isolation is harder to anchor than one spoken into a room that has already begun to lean.

## Convene only the lenses the decision touches

A pricing change is finance, marketing, sales and revenue, and legal, not HR and IT. A reorganization is HR, operations, and finance. A new data feature is IT, legal, R&D, and BI. The chair names the relevant lenses in the frame and leaves the rest out; a board of eleven on a three-function question is wasted tokens and false weight. When unsure whether a lens matters, ask the CEO during framing rather than convening it by reflex.
