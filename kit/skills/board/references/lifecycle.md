# Lifecycle - the stage model that makes every run stage-aware

*The three operating stages (FOUND, SCALE, MAINTAIN), what each one is, how to detect which one a venture is in, and how the chair scales the roster, rigor, metrics, and cadence to it. A pre-revenue idea and a mature company are not run the same way; this file is how the chair knows which one it is holding.*

The venture stage is read on **every** run, in **every** mode, during the preflight (`SKILL.md` step 0). It is not a `decide`-only or a `review`-only concern. The same machinery that would protect runway for a pre-revenue idea would strangle a mature company in process, and the same machinery that defends a mature core would let an idea die of premature scaling. Stage-awareness is the dial that keeps the standard, **every angle heard, one owner accountable**, sized to the company in front of you rather than to a generic Fortune 500. The sole-writer doctrine does not change with stage (the skill always writes, behind `write-gate.md`); what changes is who is convened, how hard they are pushed, which numbers are load-bearing, and how often the loop runs.

## The taxonomy binds to three operating stages

The venture `Overview.md` carries a fine-grained declared stage drawn from the standing taxonomy: **Idea, Validation, Pre-revenue, Early Revenue, Growth, Scaling, Established, Mature, Plateau, Decline**. That granularity is good for describing a company. It is too fine to run one, because the *operating posture* (what to protect, who to convene, how hard to push) changes at only three boundaries. So the ten declared stages collapse onto three operating stages, and the chair runs the operating stage.

| Operating stage | Taxonomy stages it covers | Funding stage | What it protects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FOUND** | Idea, Validation, Pre-revenue, (early) Early Revenue | Bootstrapped, pre-seed, Seed | Runway and learning velocity; survival, not optimization |
| **SCALE** | (late) Early Revenue, Growth, Scaling | Series A, B, C | Repeatable, efficient motion; getting the founder out of the bottleneck |
| **MAINTAIN** | Established, Mature, Plateau, Decline | Series D+, profitable, public | The core, while funding the next S-curve; guarding against bureaucracy and aging |

The seam between Early Revenue and the rest is deliberately split: a company with its first few dollars but no repeatable motion is still being **founded** (its riskiest assumption is still product-market fit), while a company with a repeatable motion it is now pouring fuel on is **scaling**. Detection (below) resolves which side of that seam a venture sits on; the declared stage string alone does not.

## FOUND (Idea to Seed)

**Funding stage:** bootstrapped through Seed. **Greiner phase:** Creativity, ending in the **Leadership crisis** (the founders can no longer run everything by feel and informal communication; the company needs a manager). **Adizes stage:** Courtship into Infancy (and the **Infant Mortality** risk: death from running out of cash or founder commitment before the product works). **Three-Horizons split:** ~100/0/0 (essentially all energy on H1, the core bet; H2 and H3 are a distraction here).

**Dominant question:** *Is there real evidence of product-market fit?* Everything else is downstream of that. The work is to validate a real, urgent problem and find the first signs of retention. The chair's job is to resist two specific temptations the founder will feel: **premature process** (writing the SOP before there is a thing to systematize) and **premature hiring** (adding headcount to a motion that is not yet repeatable). Protect runway and protect learning velocity; those are the only two resources that matter.

**Cadence:** monthly board (60 to 90 minutes, a one-page update on PMF signals, runway, and pivot decisions), a weekly Build-Measure-Learn standup, and daily founder-led customer outreach. See `operating-cadence.md` for the loop mechanics.

**Metrics that matter:** qualitative and leading. Problem-validation interviews, willingness-to-pay, activation rate, cohort retention, meaningful (not vanity) usage, and runway in months. **Suppress as not-yet-applicable:** Rule of 40, the Magic Number, CAC payback, NRR. Revenue at this stage is a lagging vanity signal, not a health signal; reporting it as a headline metric invites the company to optimize the wrong thing. The chair greys these out rather than reporting a misleadingly small or empty number (see `metrics-and-kpis.md` for the stage at which each becomes load-bearing).

## SCALE (Series A to C)

**Funding stage:** Series A through C. **Greiner phases:** Direction, then Delegation, then Coordination, crossing the **Autonomy crisis** (the centralized direction that fixed the leadership crisis now suffocates the people closest to the work) and the **Control crisis** (delegation has spread decisions so wide that the center has lost coordination). **Adizes stages:** Go-Go into Adolescence into Prime, where the **founder-to-manager transition** becomes existential and the **Founder's Trap** (the company cannot function without the founder personally) is the failure mode to name out loud. **Three-Horizons split:** ~70/20/10 (the core motion still dominates, but H2 adjacencies and H3 bets get real, protected energy).

**Dominant question:** *Is the founder the bottleneck, and is each channel actually efficient?* The work is to turn a validated motion into a repeatable, efficient one. That means naming what is actually repeatable (and what to stop doing), choosing the next VP hire by the delegation it unlocks rather than the task it offloads, evolving org design from functional toward the first divisions, and installing process **without killing speed**. The Founder's Trap and the Autonomy and Control crises are surfaced explicitly during framing, not discovered in a post-mortem.

**Cadence:** the board moves from monthly toward quarterly; a weekly "flight-check" metrics meeting runs on numbers, thresholds, and decisions, with no storytelling; quarterly OKRs recalibrate against the operating plan. See `operating-cadence.md`.

**Metrics that matter:** unit economics and efficiency. CAC payback period, Net Revenue Retention (>100% is the floor, 120%+ is elite), the burn multiple, the Magic Number, growth rate, and gross margin. The **Rule of 40** becomes the headline metric above roughly $5 to 10M ARR; below that it is noise. This is the stage where efficiency, not just growth, starts deciding whether the next round happens.

## MAINTAIN (Established to Mature, guarding against Decline)

**Funding stage:** Series D and beyond, profitable, or public. **Greiner phases:** Collaboration, then Extra-Organizational (alliances, networks, ecosystem), with the corresponding crises of **internal red tape** and the limits of the organization's own boundaries. **Adizes stages:** Prime into Stability into Aristocracy, run with **active anti-aging pressure**, because the risk has flipped: the company is no longer at risk of death-by-chaos, it is at risk of death-by-bureaucracy. **Three-Horizons split:** ~70/20/10 with H2 and H3 deliberately kept funded; the failure here is letting H1's gravity starve the next S-curve.

**Dominant question:** *Is the core defended AND is there a funded next S-curve?* The work is to allocate capital and management attention across the three horizons, defend and optimize H1 while protecting the H2 and H3 bets, and detect the earliest aging signals: loss of hunger, a drift from building innovation to buying it, and chronic R&D underinvestment. A MAINTAIN-stage chair is suspicious of its own comfort.

**Cadence:** quarterly board and quarterly OKRs, plus a recurring upkeep loop that keeps `Overview.md`, `Goals.md`, and `Log.md` current and flags stale context. See `operating-cadence.md`.

**Metrics that matter:** durability. The Rule of 40, free cash flow and margin, durable NRR, the health of the H2/H3 pipeline, and reinvestment-in-R&D as an explicit anti-aging gauge. The chair reports not just whether the core is healthy but whether the next thing is being funded; a MAINTAIN venture that scores well on H1 and empty on H2/H3 is flagged, not congratulated.

## Stage detection (signals reconciled with the declared stage)

The declared stage string in `Overview.md` is a claim, not a measurement. Founders round up (every company is "scaling"), and a stage label set six months ago goes stale. So the chair **detects** the operating stage from real signals read in the preflight, then **reconciles** the detected stage against the declared one.

Read these signals from the venture's real context (`Financials.md`, `Metrics.md`, `Hierarchy.md`, `Goals.md`, recent `Log.md`):

- **ARR / revenue:** none or pre-revenue points to FOUND; under ~$1M with an unproven motion is late FOUND; $1M to ~$30M on a repeatable motion is SCALE; tens of millions with durable margins is MAINTAIN.
- **Headcount:** a handful (under ~10) is FOUND; tens to low hundreds is SCALE; the point where Dunbar-scale coordination problems appear (~150+) and divisions exist is MAINTAIN.
- **Retention:** *whether* there is any cohort retention at all is the FOUND signal; *how durable* NRR is (>100%, 120%+) is the SCALE and MAINTAIN signal.
- **Last round and date:** Seed or none is FOUND; Series A/B/C is SCALE; Series D+, profitable, or public is MAINTAIN. A round more than ~18 months old is treated with suspicion, because the company has moved since.
- **Motion repeatability:** the seam-breaker. First dollars from founder-led, non-repeatable selling is still FOUND; a documented, repeatable, instrumented motion is SCALE regardless of the round name.

**Reconciliation rule.** When the detected stage and the declared stage agree, proceed and note it. When they **disagree**, the chair trusts the signals over the label, runs the venture at the **detected** stage, and surfaces the discrepancy: states it plainly in the run output, and (behind `write-gate.md`, autonomous since it is an internal field) updates the `Overview.md` stage with the evidence, appending the change to `Log.md`. A `review` run that finds a venture has outgrown its declared stage is exactly the moment to **advance the stage** on the record. When the signals are thin or contradictory and the gap is consequential, the chair does not guess silently: it asks one framing question (per `SKILL.md`, ask 1 to 3 framing questions when underspecified) rather than running the wrong playbook. Detection that fires on a tool-read signal beats detection that fires on a remembered label; ground the call in what `Financials.md` and `Metrics.md` actually say, not in what the founder called the company.

## How the chair scales the run to the stage

Stage is a dial on three things the chair controls, plus the metrics above. The dispatch tier (quick, standard, deep) from `dispatch.md` and the stage are orthogonal but compounding: a quick call on a FOUND-stage idea is the chair plus one department lead, while a deep call on a MAINTAIN-stage capital allocation is the full board, the dissent battery, and the Founder as decider.

- **Roster (who is convened).** FOUND convenes the **smallest sufficient** roster: often the chair (CEO) plus the one or two departments the question touches (frequently the finance skill on runway, the sales skill's ★ Chief Revenue Officer on a first-customer question, the research skill on the customer evidence, with strategy framing and the product call held by the chair). The full executive office and the committee layer are not seated for a pre-revenue idea; convening ten departments on a three-department FOUND question is wasted tokens and false weight (see `lenses.md`). SCALE widens the roster as departments become real (a sales lead distinct from the marketing seats, the finance skill's analysts on unit economics, the people skill on the org-design question). MAINTAIN can convene the full board on a governance- or money-sensitive call; the committee layer is mechanized (Audit and Risk are enforced by the git history, the repository's own checks, and the write-gate; Compensation and Nominating by the `develop` skill, the routing map, and the governance record; `workforce.md`). In all stages, convene the smallest sufficient roster for the question, then let the stage raise the ceiling on how large "sufficient" is allowed to be.

- **Rigor (how hard they are pushed).** FOUND favors speed and reversibility: a pre-mortem on the one riskiest assumption, not a full dissent battery, because the cost of a slow decision often exceeds the cost of a wrong reversible one. SCALE adds genuine dissent and verification as the decisions get more expensive and less reversible (a wrong VP hire or a wrong channel bet is costly). MAINTAIN runs the full rigor by default (the dissent battery, the independent verifier, the committee review) because at this stage the irreversible, large-blast-radius calls dominate and the company can afford the deliberation. The independent-verification doctrine (`verification.md`) never turns off; the stage sets how much of the battery fires.

- **Cadence (how often the loop runs).** FOUND runs fast and light (monthly board, weekly Build-Measure-Learn). SCALE tightens the operating cadence (the weekly flight-check, quarterly OKRs with operating-plan recalibration). MAINTAIN runs the slower governance cadence (quarterly board and OKRs) plus the upkeep loop that keeps the venture's record current. The cadence mechanics live in `operating-cadence.md`; the stage chooses which loop is the live one.

The throughline: one workforce, one standard, one sole writer, scaled to the company in front of you. The chair reads the stage before it frames the question, runs the stage-appropriate roster and rigor and cadence, reports the metrics that are load-bearing at that stage and suppresses the ones that are not yet, and advances the stage on the record when the venture has outgrown its label.
