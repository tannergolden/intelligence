# Deliverables - what the Sales & Marketing team produces, and where it goes

The department's producing map. For each deliverable: the seat that produces it, what triggers it, the completion evidence the verifier checks (its Definition of Done), and where it goes. Read it before producing anything, and put the deliverable's Definition of Done into the brief, because it is the written standard the verifier judges against.

The produce loop is fixed. The lead (the ★ Chief Revenue Officer, whom the skill embodies) frames a self-contained brief and dispatches the seat that holds the craft; the lead frames and approves and never types the deliverable itself. The seat produces and returns, with its completion evidence: every figure traced to a record, a system of record or a named source, and every assumption named. Someone other than the author verifies anything consequential (the quality-assurance skill's ★ Head of Quality). The skill writes the verified record behind its write-gate. One deliverable has one accountable owner, the ★ Chief Revenue Officer unless an entry names another.

"The venture's Sales & Marketing records" means wherever the repository's instructions keep them. The user's own deliverables (their brand, search, network and negotiations) are personal context, written only into a repository whose instructions say it is a private one made to hold it (the Constitution's SEC-003). With no such instruction, the deliverable is returned in the conversation and written only where the user asks. Inside a company-wide run, the administration skill persists that run's results.

## Standing records

The durable records the department keeps current rather than re-creates: the stage definitions and the funnel; the pipeline and the committed forecast, with its accuracy history; the revenue plan; the sales plan and playbook (stages, territory, quota); the positioning and messaging; the go-to-market surface for each product; the demand plan, its programs and its lifecycle sequences; and the pricing exception log. The pricing policy itself is a company decision, kept wherever the administration skill's decisions are kept; this skill administers it (`deal-desk.md`).

## The company's deliverables

### Positioning and messaging
- **Seat:** Marketing Manager.
- **Trigger:** a launch, a new segment, a rebrand, or a run of lost deals that points at the message.
- **Done when:** the positioning is one sentence a stranger can repeat; it names the buyer, the alternative the buyer compares against, and a true, material differentiation; the messaging maps to the buyer's jobs and drops the claims every competitor could make; every claim traces to evidence or to shipped capability; a regulated or comparative claim has passed the legal-and-compliance skill's Compliance Officer.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Sales & Marketing records (positioning). A published asset waits for the user, after the public-relations skill checks its brand voice.
- **Needs:** market and competitor facts from the research-and-development skill.

### Go-to-market (launch) plan
- **Seat:** Marketing Manager.
- **Trigger:** a launch or a new market.
- **Done when:** the ideal customer profile is specific (never "everyone"); the why-now is evidenced; the launch tiers have owners and dates; the pipeline target and the channel mix trace to a number; the readiness gate includes sales enablement (the message, the objections, the technical answers); every external asset is flagged as needing the user's confirmation (`templates/gtm-plan.md`).
- **Goes to:** the venture's Sales & Marketing records (the go-to-market surface).
- **Needs:** the positioning above, and the shipped capability the Sales Engineer confirms.

### Demand, campaign and lifecycle plan
- **Seat:** Marketing Manager.
- **Trigger:** a quarter, a launch, a pipeline gap, or a stage where leads stall (a trial that does not convert, a lead that goes quiet).
- **Done when:** the funnel math runs backward from the pipeline target to the lead volume each channel must carry; each program names its channel, its budget fit, its attribution path, and the leads, pipeline and CAC it returns; spend sits inside the CAC and payback ceiling; each lifecycle or email sequence names its entry and exit criteria, its handoff to the Account Executive, and the consent it rests on; every spend, send and contact-list upload is flagged as needing the user's confirmation.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Sales & Marketing records (the demand plan).
- **Needs:** the budget and the CAC ceiling from the company's plan (the finance-and-accounting skill), the positioning, and the legal-and-compliance skill's consent rules for marketing email.

### Deal call
- **Seat:** Account Executive, with the Sales Engineer when the deal turns on what the product can do.
- **Trigger:** a deal at a decision point, a pipeline review, or a request for a discount.
- **Done when:** the stage is named with its exit criteria met; qualification is read on need, budget, authority and timeline (or the venture's own framework); the next step and its blocker are named and dated; the offer sits inside the latitude or the exception request is framed; the close probability is weighted on evidence.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Sales & Marketing records (the pipeline), and into the forecast.

### Technical sales design and proof-of-concept plan
- **Seat:** Sales Engineer.
- **Trigger:** a buyer's technical requirements, an integration or security questionnaire, or a requested proof.
- **Done when:** each requirement maps to shipped capability or is marked a roadmap dependency; each gap is named with its workaround; the proof has success criteria, test data and steps, and can fail; a proof plan for the buyer is flagged as needing the user's confirmation before it is shared.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Sales & Marketing records (the deal).
- **Needs:** the research-and-development skill's ★ Head of Research & Development when the records do not settle what the product does; the information-technology skill for a security questionnaire's answers about how the company runs its systems.

### Pricing exception
- **Seat:** the Account Executive raises it; the ★ Chief Revenue Officer decides inside the exception band; the administration skill decides beyond it (`deal-desk.md`).
- **Trigger:** a deal that only closes outside the pricing latitude.
- **Done when:** the request carries the deal, its value and stage, the ask beside the standard offer, the give-get, the effect on price, margin and the forecast, and the precedent; the decision, its owner and its date are recorded.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Sales & Marketing records (the exception log); a decision beyond the band goes wherever the administration skill records its decisions.

### Pipeline review and forecast
- **Seat:** the Revenue Operations Manager (the stage definitions and the stage-weighted count) and the Account Executive (the deal-by-deal call), reconciled by the ★ Chief Revenue Officer (`forecast.md`, `templates/forecast.md`).
- **Trigger:** the forecast cadence the venture sets (weekly or monthly is common), a quarter, or a review run.
- **Done when:** coverage and the forecast trace to named opportunities and stated win rates from the system of record; the pipeline is dated and stage-weighted; every deal sits in a category on its evidence; the two reads are reconciled and their difference explained; the gap to target is named stage by stage, never rounded; the customer-experience skill's renewal read is included; each computed figure lists the inputs it can be recomputed from.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Sales & Marketing records (the pipeline and forecast). The committed number is the input the finance-and-accounting skill takes for the revenue line.

### Revenue plan
- **Seat:** ★ Chief Revenue Officer, with the Revenue Operations Manager.
- **Trigger:** a planning cycle.
- **Done when:** the single revenue number is reconciled across demand, the close and renewal; the stage that binds it is named; the bookings-to-revenue bridge states its timing assumptions, for the finance-and-accounting skill to apply its recognition rules; every metric references the venture's governed definitions (the business-intelligence skill) rather than redefining them.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Sales & Marketing records (the revenue plan), and to the finance-and-accounting skill for the financial model.
- **Needs:** the customer-experience skill's renewal and expansion read; the company's targets from the administration skill.

### Sales plan and playbook
- **Seat:** ★ Chief Revenue Officer, with the Account Executive (the close motion) and the Revenue Operations Manager (territory, quota, the process).
- **Trigger:** building a repeatable motion, usually at the scale stage.
- **Done when:** the close motion has named stages with exit criteria; quota is sized to demonstrated capacity and costed against the forecast; territories are balanced; the process is repeatable by someone other than the founder.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Sales & Marketing records (the sales plan).

### Revenue operations plan
- **Seat:** Revenue Operations Manager.
- **Trigger:** two tools that count the funnel differently, a new revenue tool, or a planning cycle.
- **Done when:** the stages are defined with entry and exit criteria; each figure traces to the system of record; one funnel counts each lead once across marketing, sales and success; the territory and quota design and the forecast mechanics are stated with their assumptions.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Sales & Marketing records (revenue operations). A change to the systems themselves (access, integrations, security) goes to the information-technology skill.

## The user's own deliverables (`personal.md`)

### Personal brand
- **Seat:** Marketing Manager.
- **Trigger:** a career move, a new audience to reach, a profile or a bio to rebuild, a launch of the user's own work.
- **Done when:** the positioning names the audience, the alternative the user is compared against, and a true differentiation with its evidence; the message the profile, the resume and the bio must carry is set; each program (content, outreach, speaking) names the conversations and opportunities it should return; no claim inflates a title, a number or a skill; every post and message is flagged as needing the user's confirmation, and the prose is routed to the public-relations skill.
- **Goes to:** the conversation, or the user's own records in a private repository made to hold them.

### Job search plan and tracker
- **Seat:** the ★ Chief Revenue Officer owns it; the Marketing Manager (positioning, outreach), the Revenue Operations Manager (the tracker, the funnel math), the Sales Engineer (the proof) and the Account Executive (each opportunity) produce it (`templates/job-search.md`).
- **Trigger:** "plan my job search", a layoff, a decision to move.
- **Done when:** the target is specific (the role, the level, the pay floor, the location, the date); the positioning is set; every opportunity sits in one tracker, once, at a defined stage with a dated next step; the funnel math states how many conversations the date needs at the user's own rates, or the assumed ones marked; each target role's requirements are mapped to the user's evidence, gaps named; every application and message is flagged as needing the user's confirmation.
- **Goes to:** the conversation, or the user's own records in a private repository made to hold them.
- **Needs:** pay and company facts from the research-and-development skill; career direction from the human-resources skill when the target itself is the question.

### Networking program
- **Seat:** Marketing Manager, with the Revenue Operations Manager for the list.
- **Trigger:** a search, a new field, a business to build, or a network gone quiet.
- **Done when:** the people are one list, each once, with how the user knows them and a dated next touch, kept in the user's own system; the cadence gives before it asks; each outreach is personal, true and short, and drafted unsent; the measure is conversations held and introductions made.
- **Goes to:** the conversation, or the user's own records in a private repository made to hold them.

### Negotiation plan (pay or a deal)
- **Seat:** Account Executive, with the ★ Chief Revenue Officer holding the walk-away (`templates/negotiation.md`).
- **Trigger:** a job offer, a raise, a client's rate, a purchase or a sale the user is negotiating.
- **Done when:** the target, the opening, the fallback and the walk-away are set before the talk, the walk-away being the user's own; the whole package is laid out, not one number; the opening rests on dated market evidence; every concession is traded for something; no claim is untrue (no invented offer, figure or deadline); the money is routed to the finance-and-accounting skill and the contract to the legal-and-compliance skill; the reply is drafted and marked not sent.
- **Goes to:** the conversation, or the user's own records in a private repository made to hold them.

## The producing order

Build in dependency order. When a request asks for a downstream deliverable whose upstream input is missing or stale, produce or flag the input first, and the verifier checks that the inputs were real, not assumed.

- **Go-to-market:** positioning and the ideal customer profile -> the go-to-market plan -> demand programs, lifecycle and content -> sales enablement -> the pipeline and the forecast -> (after the sale) the customer-experience skill's onboarding. Sales cannot enable what has not been positioned.
- **Measurement:** the venture's governed metric definitions (the business-intelligence skill) -> the stage definitions -> the pipeline -> the forecast. A forecast built before the stages are agreed reports numbers no one trusts.
- **Deal:** qualification -> solution fit and proof -> the offer inside policy, or the exception decided -> the close -> the handoff to the customer-experience skill (what was sold, promised and proven).
- **The user's search:** the target -> the positioning -> the tracker and the proof -> outreach and applications -> each opportunity qualified -> the negotiation -> the decision, which is the user's.

## What the department needs from its neighbours

| Need | From |
|------|------|
| Market size, competitor facts, pricing and pay benchmarks, a company's health before an interview | the research-and-development skill |
| What the product does, and what a build would take | the research-and-development skill's ★ Head of Research & Development |
| Governed metric definitions (win rate, coverage, CAC, churn), and a chart | the business-intelligence skill |
| The renewal and expansion read, account health | the customer-experience skill |
| The budget, the CAC ceiling, the revenue line in the financial model; an offer's after-tax value and equity math | the finance-and-accounting skill |
| Pricing strategy, the price list, revenue targets, a call across departments, a life decision | the administration skill's ★ Chief Executive Officer |
| A non-standard contract term, the user's own employment agreement; consent rules for marketing email; the standards gate on a claim | the legal-and-compliance skill (its Commercial Counsel; its Privacy & Data Protection Counsel; its Compliance Officer) |
| A published asset's brand voice, press for a launch, and the prose itself | the public-relations skill |
| The user's career direction and skills | the human-resources skill |
| The revenue tools' access, integrations and security | the information-technology skill |

Another department's input is read from its records where the repository's instructions keep them, or asked of the user; it is never invented. A disagreement with another department's figure goes to the administration skill's ★ Chief Executive Officer with both answers.

## Stage scaling

- **found:** a lean positioning statement, a founder-led go-to-market note, a named-deal pipeline with next steps and dates, and the evidence of willingness to pay; no weighted forecast that pretends to precision the business does not have.
- **scale:** the repeatable set: the sales plan and playbook, the full forecast discipline, revenue operations, quota and territory, and channel-level CAC.
- **maintain:** the durability set: renewal and expansion forecast with the customer-experience skill, net revenue retention read with the finance-and-accounting skill, and the positioning defended against new alternatives.
