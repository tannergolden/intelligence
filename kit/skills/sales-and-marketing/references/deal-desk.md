# Deal desk - pricing within policy, and what a deal may promise

Read before any price, discount, term or offer. The pricing policy keeps every deal honest to the company's economics; the deal desk is how a deal that does not fit the policy gets decided, by the right seat, on the record. Whatever is decided, an offer reaches the prospect only on the user's explicit confirmation. The user's own negotiations (pay or a deal) run on the same craft from the other side of the table, in `personal.md`.

## The pricing policy

- **What it holds:** the price list and the packaging, each role's discount latitude, the lead's exception band, and the standard terms (contract length, payment, renewal, the standard contract).
- **Whose it is:** setting or changing it is a company decision. The administration skill's ★ Chief Executive Officer recommends, and the user decides a major or irreversible change. This skill administers the policy and proposes changes with the evidence: win and loss reasons, the discount history, and competitor facts from the research-and-development skill.
- **With no policy on record, the latitude is zero.** Every discount and every non-standard term is out of policy and escalates, and the skill offers to draft a policy for the administration skill to decide.

## Who decides what

1. **Inside the stated latitude:** the Account Executive.
2. **Outside the latitude, inside the lead's exception band:** the Chief Revenue Officer, on the record with its reason.
3. **Beyond the band, with no policy on record, a precedent-setting deal (a new price point, a most-favoured-customer clause, a multi-year price lock), or any change to the price list or the policy:** the administration skill's ★ Chief Executive Officer, and the user on a major or irreversible call.
4. **A non-standard contract term** (liability, indemnity, data protection, payment beyond the standard terms): the legal-and-compliance skill's Commercial Counsel reviews it before anyone agrees to it.
5. **Whatever rung decided it,** the offer, the quote or the contract reaches the prospect only on the user's explicit confirmation in the conversation.

## What an exception request carries

- The deal, its value and stage, and why it closes only this way (the competing alternative, the buyer's budget evidence).
- The ask (the discount, the term) set beside the standard offer.
- **The give-get:** what the company receives in return, such as a longer term, more volume, prepayment, a reference or a case study, or a faster close. No concession without a trade.
- The effect on price, margin and the forecast, and the precedent it sets for the next deal.
- The decision, its owner and its date, recorded in the exception log with the deal.

## Qualify before pricing

- A deal is priced only once it is qualified (need, budget, authority and timeline, or the venture's own framework); a discount on an unqualified deal buys nothing.
- A disqualifying fact ends the chase: the deal is closed lost with its reason, which feeds the positioning and the forecast's win rate.

## What a deal may promise

- **Only shipped capability as present fact.** A roadmap item is named as one, with no date the research-and-development skill's ★ Head of Research & Development has not committed (the Sales Engineer's rule).
- **Only the terms the contract carries.** No side letters, and no spoken commitment outside the order form.
- **Only claims the positioning can support** with evidence; a regulated or comparative claim (a health, financial, savings or "better than" claim) passes the legal-and-compliance skill's Compliance Officer before it appears in a proposal or an asset.

## Renewals and expansion

Renewal pricing and expansion deals are this skill's, run on the customer-experience skill's health read. A discount to save an at-risk renewal climbs the same ladder as any other, with the health evidence attached and the customer-experience skill's view of whether price is the real cause; a discount that papers over an unsolved problem only moves the churn to the next renewal.

## Personal data

A prospect's contact details live in the company's system of record and are read in place. Uploading a list to an ad platform, enriching it through a vendor, or handing it to anyone outside the company is sharing personal data, and waits for the user's confirmation; the consent and unsubscribe rules for marketing email are checked with the legal-and-compliance skill's Privacy & Data Protection Counsel.
