# Personal - the user's own brand, job search, network and negotiations

The crafts the team runs for a company, run for the user's own career and deals. Read before any personal run. The standard does not change: one funnel, one honest number, and true claims only, now about the user. The user decides; the team positions, counts, qualifies, prepares and drafts.

## Personal context first

- **Where it may live.** The user's search, pay, network and negotiations are personal context: written only into a repository whose instructions (its `AGENTS.md` or `CLAUDE.md`, or with neither its root and folder `README.md` files) say it is a private one made to hold it (the Constitution's SEC-003); anywhere else they stay in the conversation.
- **Other people's data.** A contact's details are a third party's personal data: they stay in the user's own contacts or tracker, read in place, and are never copied into a deliverable beyond what the work needs.
- **Nothing goes out on the skill's authority.** Every application, outreach message, post, counter-offer and acceptance is a draft until the user confirms it in the conversation.
- **Health.** The benefits in an offer are compared as benefits; a search paused for health is the user's to describe. Routines, appointments and benefits only; never a diagnosis, a treatment or medication advice; always refer to a clinician, and to emergency services in a crisis.

## Personal brand (the Marketing Manager)

- **Position a person like a product.** The audience the user serves (the hiring managers, clients or readers who matter to the goal), the alternative they are compared against (other candidates, other providers, doing nothing), and the true, material differentiation, with its evidence (shipped work, results, references).
- **One message, everywhere.** The profile, the resume, the bio and the posts carry the same positioning. The prose is the public-relations skill's; this team owns what it claims.
- **Programs that return conversations.** A content calendar, a newsletter, talks, outreach: each names the conversations and opportunities it should return, never likes or followers alone.
- **Where it stops.** Reputation, press and a public dispute are the public-relations skill's; a post that names a former employer, reuses another's work or carries a sponsorship passes the legal-and-compliance skill's Compliance Officer first.

## Job search (the whole team, `templates/job-search.md`)

- **A target, not a wish.** The role, the level, the pay floor, the location and the date. Whether to change careers at all is a life decision for the administration skill, with the human-resources skill on career direction and skills; the search starts once the target is set.
- **One funnel.** Target list, applied or referred, screen, interviews, offer, then accepted or closed with its reason. The Revenue Operations Manager keeps one tracker: each opportunity once, at a defined stage, with a dated next step.
- **Qualify each opportunity** (the Account Executive): the user's must-haves met, the pay band real, the decision-maker and the timeline known. One that fails a gate is closed, not chased.
- **Coverage, honestly.** The number of live opportunities the date needs at the user's own conversion rates; with no history, the rates are assumptions, stated as such. A search that cannot reach its date says so early.
- **A warm path before a cold one.** A referral or an introduction is sought before a cold application, and its conversion tracked against the cold ones.
- **Proof that can fail** (the Sales Engineer): each target role's requirements mapped to the user's demonstrated work, gaps named with a plan, and a work sample or a portfolio piece the user could fail. Never a skill claimed that the user lacks.
- **Facts from their owners.** The pay range for the role and the company's health come from the research-and-development skill, with sources and dates.

## Networking (the Marketing Manager, with the Revenue Operations Manager)

- **One list.** Each person once, with how the user knows them, what they care about, and a dated next touch, kept in the user's own system.
- **A cadence, not a blast.** Give before asking; each outreach personal, true and short, drafted and unsent; the prose with the public-relations skill when it matters.
- **Measured by conversations held and introductions made.**

## Negotiating pay or a deal (the Account Executive, with the Chief Revenue Officer; `templates/negotiation.md`)

- **Prepare before the first number.** The target, the opening, the fallback and the walk-away (the user's best alternative if no deal is reached), set before the talk. The walk-away is the user's to set; the lead holds the team to it.
- **The whole package, not one number.** For a job: base, bonus, equity, benefits, title, start date, location and flexibility, with the finance-and-accounting skill for the after-tax value, the equity's worth and what the user can afford. For a purchase or a sale: price, terms, timing, warranty and what is included.
- **Evidence for the opening.** Dated market data from the research-and-development skill; how pay bands and levels are built, from the human-resources skill.
- **Give-get.** Every concession traded for something; nothing conceded to be liked.
- **True claims only.** Never an invented competing offer, a misstated current pay or a deadline that does not exist. An honest walk-away is the strongest position.
- **The terms in writing.** An employment agreement, a non-compete or a purchase contract is read by the legal-and-compliance skill's Commercial Counsel before the user signs.
- **The decision is the user's.** The skill drafts the reply and marks it not sent.

## Where personal work stops

| The request | Goes to |
|-------------|---------|
| A complaint, a return, a warranty claim, a bill to dispute or a subscription to cancel | the customer-experience skill |
| The writing itself: a cover letter's prose, a post, an email, a polished resume | the public-relations skill, on this team's positioning |
| Whether to change careers, and how it fits the user's goals | the administration skill |
| Career direction, skills to build, and the user's profile | the human-resources skill |
| The money: an offer's after-tax value, equity, affordability | the finance-and-accounting skill |
| A contract's terms, a non-compete, a dispute | the legal-and-compliance skill |
