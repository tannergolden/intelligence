# Deliverables - what the Customer Experience team produces, and where it goes

The department's producing map, for a company's customers and for the user as a customer. For each deliverable: the seat that produces it, what triggers it, the completion evidence the verifier checks (its Definition of Done), and where it goes. Read it before producing anything, and put the deliverable's Definition of Done into the brief, because it is the written standard the verifier judges against.

The produce loop is fixed. The lead (the ★ Chief Customer Officer, whom the skill embodies) frames a self-contained brief and dispatches the seat that holds the craft; the lead frames and approves and never types the deliverable itself. The seat produces and returns, with its completion evidence: every claim traced to the ticket, the account's records or a named source, and every assumption named. Someone other than the author verifies anything consequential (the quality-assurance skill's ★ Head of Quality). The skill writes the verified record behind its write-gate. One deliverable has one accountable owner, the ★ Chief Customer Officer unless an entry names another.

"The venture's Customer Experience records" means wherever the repository's instructions keep them. The user's own claims, subscriptions and warranties are their personal context, kept only in a repository whose instructions (its `AGENTS.md` or `CLAUDE.md`, or with neither its root and folder `README.md` files) say it is a private one made to hold it (the Constitution's SEC-003). With no such instruction, the deliverable is returned in the conversation and written only where the user asks. Inside a company-wide run, the administration skill persists that run's results. A customer's personal data never goes into these records beyond what the work needs, and no record holds a card or account number, a password or a sign-in.

## Standing records

The durable records the department keeps current rather than re-creates: the onboarding and success playbooks; the customer health and renewal record; the support SLAs and their attainment; the ticket-theme counts; the knowledge base; the escalation log; and the voice-of-the-customer record.

## The deliverables

### Onboarding playbook
- **Seat:** Customer Success Manager.
- **Trigger:** a new motion, a new segment, or onboarding that keeps stalling.
- **Done when:** each module has an owner, a time box and a measurable first-value milestone per segment; the handoff from the sales-and-marketing skill (what was sold, promised and proven) is defined; completion is measured at first value, never at kickoff.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Customer Experience records (playbooks).

### Account onboarding plan
- **Seat:** Customer Success Manager.
- **Trigger:** a newly signed customer.
- **Done when:** the customer's stated goal and its first-value milestone are named and dated; each step has an owner on both sides; what the deal promised, from the sales-and-marketing skill's handoff, is listed and checked against what the product ships, with any gap raised at once; the risks are named.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Customer Experience records (the account). Every message to the customer is a draft until the user confirms it.

### Account health read and renewal review
- **Seat:** Customer Success Manager.
- **Trigger:** an account entering its renewal-risk window (`health.md`), a health signal turning, or the quarterly review.
- **Done when:** health is read from realized value against the customer's goal, each signal with its evidence; the next value step is named; the renewal or expansion call carries its risk and its play; an at-risk renewal is flagged inside its window, never at the date; an expansion signal is handed to the sales-and-marketing skill with its evidence (`templates/account-review.md`).
- **Goes to:** the venture's Customer Experience records (health and renewals). The sales-and-marketing skill takes renewal pricing and the expansion deal, and its forecast takes the renewal read.

### Customer success plan
- **Seat:** Customer Success Manager.
- **Trigger:** building retention, usually at the scale stage.
- **Done when:** the health score is defined from realized value, with its signals and thresholds; the renewal calendar and its risk window are set; the plays for risk, recovery, renewal and expansion are named with owners; retention metrics reference the venture's governed definitions (the business-intelligence skill) rather than redefining them.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Customer Experience records (playbooks).

### Ticket resolution
- **Seat:** Support Specialist.
- **Trigger:** an inbound issue.
- **Done when:** the root cause is diagnosed, or reproduced, before a fix is offered; the fix is given, or the escalation carries the diagnosis and the reproduction; the severity and impact are stated; the closing reply is drafted and marked not sent, and promises only what an owner has committed; any pattern is noted with its count (`templates/resolution.md`).
- **Goes to:** the conversation, and the venture's Customer Experience records (the ticket log and theme counts). The reply goes to the customer only on the user's confirmation.

### Pattern escalation
- **Seat:** the Support Specialist (the diagnosis, the reproduction and the count); the ★ Chief Customer Officer calls it a pattern and routes it (`escalation.md`).
- **Trigger:** the same cause in more than one account, a ticket that keeps recurring, or any severity-1 issue.
- **Done when:** the pattern carries its count, the affected accounts, the diagnosis, the reproduction and the customer impact; it is routed to the one owning skill the routing table names; each affected customer has a drafted reply naming the next step and when they will hear again.
- **Goes to:** the owning skill (handed over by the user, or carried by the administration skill's Chief of Staff in a company-wide run); the venture's Customer Experience records (the escalation log).

### Knowledge-base article
- **Seat:** Support Specialist.
- **Trigger:** a resolved issue other customers are likely to hit, or a how-to that keeps being asked.
- **Done when:** the article states the symptom in the customer's words, the cause, the fix or workaround step by step, and the product version it applies to; it holds no customer's data; a public article is flagged as needing the user's confirmation before it is published.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Customer Experience records (knowledge-base drafts). Publishing is gated.

### Support SLAs
- **Seat:** Support Specialist.
- **Trigger:** building support at the scale stage, a new support tier, or SLAs that keep being missed.
- **Done when:** response and resolution targets are numbers per severity; attainment is tracked against them; ticket themes are counted and fed to the voice of the customer.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Customer Experience records (SLAs). An SLA written into a customer's contract is the legal-and-compliance skill's to review and the user's to agree.

### Voice-of-the-customer report
- **Seat:** ★ Chief Customer Officer, from the Support Specialist's ticket themes and the Customer Success Manager's account themes.
- **Trigger:** the cadence the venture sets (monthly or quarterly is common), or ahead of a planning cycle.
- **Done when:** each theme carries its count, its trend, its evidence (anonymized verbatims), the accounts or revenue affected, and the one owning skill it was routed to; feature requests are recorded as evidence, never as commitments; the customers to tell when a theme is resolved are listed for drafted replies (`templates/voc-report.md`).
- **Goes to:** the venture's Customer Experience records. Each theme goes to its owning department, and a product decision to the administration skill's ★ Chief Executive Officer.

### Quarterly customer review
- **Seat:** ★ Chief Customer Officer.
- **Trigger:** the company's quarterly review.
- **Done when:** retention (net and gross revenue retention, logo churn), the health distribution, the renewals ahead, the support load and SLA attainment, and the top voice-of-the-customer themes are stated against the venture's governed definitions, each number traced to its source.
- **Goes to:** the venture's Customer Experience records, and to the administration skill's quarterly review.

## For the user as a customer

These deliverables serve the user's own life (`consumer.md`). Each goes only to a private repository made to hold the user's personal context, where its instructions put it; anywhere else it stays in the conversation, with its deadlines listed for the user to keep.

### Complaint, return, refund request or warranty claim
- **Seat:** Support Specialist; the ★ Chief Customer Officer picks the remedy and the ladder.
- **Trigger:** something the user bought, or a service they paid for, failed, arrived wrong, was not delivered, or was billed wrongly, and the user wants it put right.
- **Done when:** the facts are stated (what, from whom, when, the order or receipt by where it lives, never a card number); the problem is in one line with its evidence; the remedy asked is specific (a refund, a replacement, a repair, a credit, a redo) and its basis is named (the seller's policy or the warranty, fetched from the seller's own page and dated; what the law may add flagged for the legal-and-compliance skill, never asserted); the deadlines are dated (the return window, the warranty's end, the reply due); the draft is addressed to the right first rung, marked not sent, and asks for a written reply; the next rung if it is refused is named (`templates/claim.md`).
- **Goes to:** the conversation, or the user's own records in a private repository made to hold them. Sending it is the user's act.

### Escalation of a refused claim
- **Seat:** ★ Chief Customer Officer, with the Support Specialist's facts.
- **Trigger:** the seller refused, ignored the deadline, or offered less than the user is owed.
- **Done when:** the refusal is recorded as given; the next rung is chosen (the seller's formal complaints route, an ombudsman or regulator where one covers the seller, a card dispute the user files, a small claim), with its trigger, its deadline and what it costs the user in time or fees; anything that turns on the law goes to the legal-and-compliance skill, which says when a licensed lawyer must review; the money side of a card dispute goes to the finance-and-accounting skill; the point where the next rung costs more than what is at stake is named.
- **Goes to:** the conversation, or the user's own records in a private repository made to hold them.

### Subscriptions audit and calendar
- **Seat:** Customer Success Manager.
- **Trigger:** the user asks what they pay for, a renewal or a price rise is coming, or money is tight.
- **Done when:** each subscription and service plan is listed with its cost as the user reports it, how often it is used, its renewal date and its cancel-by date; each carries a call (keep, downgrade, renegotiate or cancel) with its reason; each change carries the provider's own steps, fetched and dated; every cancellation or change is listed as the user's act, with the date by which it must happen; no card number or sign-in is recorded (`templates/subscriptions.md`).
- **Goes to:** the conversation, or the user's own records in a private repository made to hold them. The household budget it feeds is the finance-and-accounting skill's.

### Return windows and warranties record
- **Seat:** Customer Success Manager.
- **Trigger:** a significant purchase, or the user wants to stop losing claims to missed dates.
- **Done when:** each purchase has its return-window end, its warranty end and what the warranty covers, where its receipt and warranty document live, and the claim steps; the dates nearest to closing are flagged first (`templates/subscriptions.md`).
- **Goes to:** only a private repository made to hold the user's personal context; otherwise the conversation, for the user to keep.

## The producing order

Build in dependency order. When a request asks for a downstream deliverable whose upstream input is missing or stale, produce or flag the input first, and the verifier checks that the inputs were real, not assumed.

- **After the sale:** the sales-and-marketing skill's handoff (what was sold, promised and proven) -> the account onboarding plan -> first value -> the health read -> the renewal review -> renewal and expansion, with the sales-and-marketing skill.
- **Support:** severity and triage -> diagnosis -> resolution or escalation -> the closing reply -> the knowledge-base article -> the theme counts -> the voice-of-the-customer report.
- **The user as a customer:** the facts and the seller's policy -> the remedy -> the claim to the first rung -> the reply or its deadline -> the next rung, until the remedy lands; and the subscriptions and warranties record -> the calendar -> each change before its date.
- **Measurement:** the venture's governed metric definitions (the business-intelligence skill) -> the health score -> the success plan and the quarterly customer review.

## What the department needs from its neighbours

| Need | From |
|------|------|
| The deal handoff: what was sold, promised, priced and proven | the sales-and-marketing skill |
| Renewal pricing, the expansion deal, a discount | the sales-and-marketing skill's ★ Chief Revenue Officer |
| A product defect owned, verified and its fix routed | the quality-assurance skill's ★ Head of Quality |
| Internal tools, access, an internal outage, the helpdesk's plumbing; the user's devices and accounts | the information-technology skill's ★ Chief Information Officer |
| A security incident or a suspected breach | the information-technology skill, with the legal-and-compliance skill's Privacy & Data Protection Counsel for personal data |
| Refunds, credits, billing errors, collections; the money side of the user's card dispute, and the household budget | the finance-and-accounting skill |
| A contract dispute, a data-subject request, a contractual SLA; what the law entitles the user to, a small claim | the legal-and-compliance skill |
| A public incident, a complaint gone public, press | the public-relations skill's ★ Chief Communications Officer |
| A handoff that keeps breaking across teams; a contractor held to the scope of work | the operations-and-logistics skill's ★ Head of Operations |
| A customer research study (interviews, a designed survey) | the research-and-development skill |
| Governed metric definitions (retention, churn, CSAT, NPS) | the business-intelligence skill |
| A product decision drawn from feedback | the administration skill's ★ Chief Executive Officer |
| A polished reply, a chart | the public-relations skill, the business-intelligence skill |

Another department's input is read from its records where the repository's instructions keep them, or asked of the user; it is never invented. A disagreement with another department goes to the administration skill's ★ Chief Executive Officer with both answers.

## Stage scaling

- **found:** the founder close to every customer; a simple onboarding checklist to first value; every ticket read for what it says about product-market fit; retention evidence over retention metrics.
- **scale:** the repeatable set: the onboarding playbook, the success plan and its health score, SLAs per severity, the knowledge base, and the voice-of-the-customer cadence.
- **maintain:** the durability set: net and gross revenue retention defended account by account, renewal cohorts forecast with the sales-and-marketing skill, and the knowledge base kept current with every release.

The user as a customer has no stage: scale the work to what is at stake, a short claim for a small refund, the full ladder for a costly failure.
