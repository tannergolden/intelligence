# Health - reading an account, securing the renewal, and the voice of the customer

Read before an account's health read, a renewal review, an onboarding plan, or the voice-of-the-customer report. The rule underneath it: realized value, not activity, is an account's health, and silence on an account is a signal, not safety. The same rule reads the user's own subscriptions, in `consumer.md`.

## Read health from realized value

Score each signal green, amber or red with its evidence, and never reduce an account to one unexplained number:

- **Value realized:** is the customer reaching the goal it bought for? The strongest signal, weighed above the rest.
- **Adoption depth:** are the workflows that deliver that goal in use, by the people who need them? Logins without outcomes are a red flag.
- **Relationship:** is the sponsor still there and engaged, or has the champion left?
- **Support friction:** open severity-1 or severity-2 issues, repeat tickets, the satisfaction trend.
- **Commercial:** payment on time, a request to cut seats or scope, a procurement review.
- **Sentiment:** the trend of the recommendation score, and what customers say in their own words.

Where the venture has its own health score, use it and report its signals the same way. The metric definitions (net and gross revenue retention, churn, CSAT, NPS, time to first value) are the venture's governed ones, owned by the business-intelligence skill; this team uses them and never redefines them.

## Onboarding ends at first value

- **Each segment has a first-value milestone:** the first moment the customer gets the outcome it bought, named and dated in the onboarding plan.
- **Onboarding is measured by time to first value** and the share of customers who reach it, never by kickoff calls held.
- **The plan starts from the sales-and-marketing skill's handoff:** what was sold, promised and proven. A promise the product does not keep is raised at once, not discovered at the renewal.

## The renewal calendar

- **Every renewal has a date and a risk window before it:** 90 days for an annual contract and a third of the term for a shorter one by default, unless the venture sets its own.
- **An account red or amber inside its window** gets a recovery play with an owner and a date at once; a renewal lost in silence is the failure this calendar prevents.
- **A renewal at risk is reported as at risk** to the sales-and-marketing skill's forecast, never as safe.

## Renewal before expansion

- **Secure an at-risk renewal before any expansion is chased,** and expand only a healthy, value-realizing account; expanding an unhappy one speeds the churn.
- **An expansion signal** (a need a higher tier, more seats or another product meets) goes to the sales-and-marketing skill with its evidence; the deal and its price are that skill's.
- **A discount to save a renewal** is the sales-and-marketing skill's to decide, with this team's view of whether price is the real cause; a discount over an unsolved problem only moves the churn to the next renewal.

## The voice of the customer

- **Gather themes from every channel the team touches:** tickets, success conversations, onboarding friction, surveys, and the reasons for churn and downgrades.
- **Count, trend and route them:** attach anonymized evidence, size the accounts or revenue affected, and route each theme to the one department that owns it (`escalation.md`).
- **A feature request is evidence for a product decision, never a promise** to the customer; the product decision is the administration skill's ★ Chief Executive Officer's.
- **Close the loop outward:** when a theme is resolved or a request ships, the customers who raised it are told, through drafted replies the user confirms.
- **A customer research study** (an interview program, a designed survey) is the research-and-development skill's; this team supplies its themes as the starting evidence.
