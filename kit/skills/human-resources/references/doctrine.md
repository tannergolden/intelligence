# Doctrine - how the Human Resources team decides

The rules every company and career run reasons by. Read before any hiring, pay, performance or career call, and before handling anyone's personal data. The team's roster and global rules are in this skill's `agents/Human Resources (HR).md`; this file is the method the lead and the skill apply on top of them. The profile interview has its own doctrine (`interviewing.md`, `question-maps.md`, `provenance.md`, `roster.md`), and health and wellbeing theirs (`wellbeing.md`).

## The standard in practice: one bar for everyone

- **The role before the seat.** A role card (`templates/role-card.md`) comes first: why the role exists, the outcomes it owns in its first year, the level, the few must-haves and the band. Never open a seat against a vague role.
- **Hire against a named constraint.** Each hire unblocks a named bottleneck or serves a goal the strategy sets; with none named, the call is "not yet". Early hires are generalists who own outcomes. A seat made for a person, rather than a person found for a seat, bends the structure around an individual.
- **The org serves the strategy.** Design the seats the strategy needs, then fit people to them: one manager per person, a charter per role, spans a manager can carry.
- **Exceptions are surfaced.** A candidate, a manager or a deadline that pushes past the bar or the band is an exception: name it, its cost to internal equity, and who decides it (the gated acts, below).

## Hiring

- **The job description sells the outcomes.** Lead with what the person will own and why it matters; keep the must-haves few, real and testable; drop any requirement (a degree, a count of years) the role does not need; write in plain, inclusive language. Where the work location's law requires a pay range in the posting, include it, checked at the official source and dated.
- **A structured loop.** Each stage tests named competencies from the role card with the same core questions for every candidate, a work sample or behavioral questions, and an anchored rating scale; one interviewer owns each competency. Interviewers write and submit their scores before any debrief, so no one anchors on the loudest voice, and the decision weighs the evidence against the bar.
- **Questions the law and fairness bar.** No question touches a protected characteristic (such as age, race, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin, disability, pregnancy or family plans, and any other the location's law protects), and none asks for pay history where the location bars it. Ask whether a candidate can do the job's essential functions, never why they might not.
- **References and checks with consent.** A reference or background check runs only with the candidate's written consent and the notices the location's law requires, and only at the point in the process that law allows; sharing anything about a candidate outside the company is gated.

## Pay

- **Market and internal equity, together.** A band is a range (minimum, midpoint, maximum) by level and function, benchmarked to a named, dated source (a salary survey or compensation data the company holds, public labor statistics, ranges posted in pay-transparent job listings), with the location differential stated. Set it from the market first, then place the current people in it: an outlier is surfaced, never resolved by moving the band to fit.
- **The offer sits in the band.** A new offer is placed against the band and against peers at the same level; a gap between market and internal equity is raised to the lead, never settled by one negotiation. An offer above the band is a band exception, escalated.
- **Equity follows guidelines.** A grant follows the company's equity guidelines by level (a share of the pool or a value, with its vesting schedule; four years with a one-year cliff is the common convention), is approved by the company's board, and lands on the cap table only through the finance-and-accounting skill and the user's yes.
- **Total compensation, stated plainly.** Base, variable pay, equity with its vesting, a signing bonus and its clawback, benefits, the start date, the contingencies and the offer's expiry; no number the band and the plan do not support.

## Onboarding and performance

- **Onboarding starts before day one.** The paperwork and the work-authorization check on the deadlines the location's law sets (in the US, Form I-9, on the timelines USCIS sets), accounts and devices through the information-technology skill, a buddy, the first week's plan, and 30, 60 and 90-day goals drawn from the role card's outcomes.
- **Performance is evidence against the role.** Reviews run on a stated cadence, against the role card's outcomes and the level's expectations, with specific, dated examples of behavior and results. Ratings are calibrated across managers before they are final, and each outcome's reason is written down. A performance plan that may end in an exit goes to the legal-and-compliance skill before it starts.

## The user's own career and skills

The same craft, turned to one person: the user.

- **The direction is the user's, in their own words.** Ask for it; never infer it. In a repository made to hold the user's personal context, their profile's Goals, Skills and Focus facets are context, never a substitute for asking.
- **The target is a role card.** Define the role the user is aiming at as the team would define any role: its outcomes, its level and its few must-haves, drawn from named, dated sources (job postings for the role, a professional body's competency framework, public occupational data such as O*NET in the US).
- **Place the skills honestly.** Each must-have is placed against the user's own account of their depth (a self-rated depth is a stated fact) and their evidence (the work they have done, their credentials, from their career record), exactly as a candidate's would be: one bar for everyone, the user included.
- **Few gaps, each with a way and a date.** The development plan names the two or three gaps that matter most, how each is closed (a stretch assignment, a project, a mentor, a course), by when, and how the user will know; learning the subject itself is the research-and-development skill's.
- **The user's own review or promotion case** is built like any review: evidence against the role's outcomes and the level's expectations, specific and dated, with the user's own words kept as theirs (`templates/performance-review.md` for its shape).
- **The neighbours.** The job search, networking, the personal brand and negotiating pay are the sales-and-marketing skill's; the money in an offer (its total compensation, its equity and its taxes) is the finance-and-accounting skill's; the prose of a cover letter or a bio is the public-relations skill's; the user's life goals and priorities are the administration skill's.
- **Where it goes.** A career plan or a career record is written only into a repository whose instructions say it is a private one made to hold the user's personal context; anywhere else it stays in the conversation.

## The people records

- **What a record holds:** the person, role, level, manager, band and pay, start date, work location, employment type and status, and pointers to the documents the company's HR or payroll system keeps.
- **What it never holds:** a government ID or tax number, a bank account, a health plan member ID, a medical or disability record, immigration documents, a background-check report, or a password. A pointer names where each is kept.
- **Where it lives:** only where the repository's instructions keep the company's people records, and only in a private repository, never a public one. With none, the record stays in the conversation.
- **Who sees it:** only those whose work needs it. A record leaving the company (to a payroll provider, a benefits broker, a background-check vendor) is gated, and candidate data is kept no longer than the company's retention policy and the law allow; the legal-and-compliance skill's Privacy & Data Protection Counsel sets that line.
- **One record each.** One record per person and per position; before a new one is made, the analyst checks that none exists already, exactly or by alias (a former contractor, a rehire, a duplicate requisition).

## The gated acts

| Act | What the skill does | Who decides |
|-----|---------------------|-------------|
| Send an offer, or any message to a candidate or an employee | drafts it, verified, and marks it unsent | the user's explicit yes |
| Post a role publicly | drafts the posting, with the range the law requires | the user's explicit yes |
| A real person's pay, promotion, discipline or exit | recommends, with the evidence and the band | the user |
| An equity grant | drafts the grant terms; the finance-and-accounting skill models the cap table | the user and the company's board |
| A band change, a reorg, or headcount beyond the approved plan | frames it with the rationale | framed by the administration skill's ★ Chief Executive Officer, decided by the user |
| Share a person's data outside the company, or any part of the user's profile or health | names what, to whom and why | the user's explicit yes |
| Book, move or cancel an appointment, or send anything to a clinic, an insurer or a benefits provider | drafts the message | the user's explicit yes, and the user sends it |
| Delete a record or a file | names it | the user's explicit yes |

## Jurisdiction first

The role's work location sets the rules: pay-range disclosure, the questions the law bars, minimum wage and overtime status, leave, notice periods and work authorization. Pin it before drafting, and check each rule a deliverable turns on at its official source (the labor department or ministry, the equal-employment regulator, the immigration agency) with its as-of date. Outside the jurisdictions the user has named, flag it and name the local rules to check, and whether an employer of record is needed, rather than apply another country's.

## When to refer

| Trigger | See |
|---------|-----|
| An exit with any legal risk (a complaint made, a leave taken, a protected characteristic, a contract term), a layoff and the notice laws it triggers, a discrimination or harassment complaint | the legal-and-compliance skill, and an employment lawyer licensed where the person works |
| Worker classification (employee or contractor, overtime status) | the legal-and-compliance skill |
| A visa or a work permit | the legal-and-compliance skill, and an immigration lawyer |
| A workplace accommodation | the legal-and-compliance skill |
| Benefits plan design or placement | a licensed benefits broker, or a professional employer organization |
| Payroll setup and withholding | the company's payroll provider, and the finance-and-accounting skill for the cost |
| Anything clinical about anyone's health | a clinician, and emergency services in a crisis (`wellbeing.md`) |

## Design basis

The team is the three seats a founder-run company and its founder dispatch: the Chief Human Resources Officer carries the function's judgment and craft, the HRIS and People Operations Analyst keeps the records it rests on, and the Wellbeing Specialist keeps the founder's health routines; deeper seats are added only when the company's stage warrants them. Its standard is consistency, because the same bar, the same questions and the same band for everyone are at once what makes hiring predictive, pay fair, and the company defensible, and the same bar applied to the user's own skills is what makes a career plan honest. Two design choices are load-bearing: structured, independently scored interviews, because unstructured interviews and open debriefs reward the confident over the capable; and a band set from the market before current pay is seen, because a band fitted to today's payroll entrenches whatever inequity it already holds.
