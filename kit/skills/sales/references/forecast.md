# Forecast - one funnel, one forecast

The forecast is the number the rest of the company plans on: the finance skill's revenue line, hiring, and spend all rest on it. A forecast shaded up to please becomes a planning error the whole company inherits, so the team's standard, one funnel and one honest number, is strictest here. Read before any pipeline review, forecast call or revenue plan; the shape of the call is `templates/forecast.md`.

## Define the funnel before counting it

- **One set of stages for the whole engine,** each with an entry and an exit criterion the buyer can verify (a meeting held, a need confirmed, a proof passed, a proposal received), never a seller's activity alone.
- **One system of record.** Where the CRM, the marketing tool and a spreadsheet disagree, the system of record wins and the difference is named, never summed.
- **A lead is counted once,** from first touch through the close to the renewal, with each handoff defined: demand to sales, and sales to the customers skill's team.
- **A default where the venture has none,** stated as a default: Lead; Qualified (need, budget, authority and timeline confirmed); Discovery done (the problem and the buyer's decision process mapped); Solution validated (the demo or the proof passed against the buyer's criteria); Proposal (pricing and terms in the buyer's hands); Commit (a dated, mutual close plan, with terms inside policy or approved); Closed won, or Closed lost with its reason.

## Two reads, made blind, then reconciled

- **The deal-by-deal call** (the Account Executive): each open deal placed in a category on its evidence.
- **The stage-weighted count** (the Revenue Operations Manager): open, dated pipeline times each stage's historical conversion rate from the system of record, over a stated window.
- The two are made blind to each other, and the lead reconciles them. Where they differ by more than the run's tolerance (10 percent by default), the difference is a finding, explained deal by deal, never averaged away.

## Forecast categories

- **Commit:** the deal clears every qualification gate, sits at the Commit stage, has a dated next step the buyer agreed to, and carries terms inside policy or already approved. The team would stake its record on it.
- **Best case:** qualified, with a known blocker and a dated plan to clear it.
- **Pipeline:** qualified and open, too early to call for the period.
- **Omitted:** unqualified, stalled, or closing outside the period.

A deal moves up a category only on new evidence, never on a seller's renewed confidence.

## Coverage, slippage and age

- **Coverage** is open qualified pipeline divided by the target still to close in the period. The coverage needed is about one divided by the venture's own win rate (a 25 percent win rate needs about 4x). With no win-rate history, 3x to 4x is the common rule of thumb, stated as an assumption.
- **A close date in the past is a defect,** fixed before the call is made.
- **Slippage downgrades.** A deal whose close date has moved twice, or that has sat past its stage's typical age, drops one category until new evidence moves it back.
- **Time left binds.** Pipeline whose usual cycle is longer than the time left in the period cannot save the period, and is shown apart from what can.

## The constrained stage binds

The revenue number spans demand, the close and renewal. Where they disagree on what converts, the stage with the weakest evidence-backed rate binds the number, not the most optimistic one. Renewal and expansion come from the customers skill's health read: a renewal its team rates at risk is never in commit, and an expansion counts only once there is a qualified deal for it.

## Never round up to the target

A forecast below target ships below target. The gap is framed stage by stage (too little pipeline, a weak win rate, slipped deals, renewals at risk), with the moves that could close it, and escalated: a move inside the plan is the Chief Revenue Officer's to make; a gap no move inside the plan closes goes to the board skill's ★ Chief Executive Officer, and to the user when the company's plan rests on the number.

## Commit is a promise, and accuracy is kept

- **The committed number changes only on evidence,** and every change names the deals that moved it.
- **Computed numbers are recomputed, not recalled.** Every sum, ratio and weighted total lists its inputs, so the verifier can redo it.
- **Accuracy is tracked.** Each period closes with the forecast set against the actual, by category, and the error recorded with the period's records; a category that misses in the same direction twice gets its rule tightened.
- **The handoff to finance.** The committed forecast is the input the finance skill takes for the revenue line; finance owns the model and its recognition rules, sales owns the number's evidence.

## Stage scaling

- **found:** a list of named deals with their next steps and dates, and the evidence of willingness to pay (pilots, letters of intent, pre-orders); no weighted forecast that pretends to precision the business does not have.
- **scale:** the full discipline above: stages, categories, the two blind reads, coverage, slippage, accuracy tracking.
- **maintain:** add renewal cohorts and expansion from the customers skill, and net revenue retention, read with the finance skill.

The metric definitions the forecast uses (win rate, coverage, cycle length, CAC) are the venture's governed definitions, owned by the analytics skill; the forecast uses them and never redefines them.
