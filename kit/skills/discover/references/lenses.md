# Lens library - distinct angles for divergent generation

A library of generative lenses. The lead assigns each Idea Generator one distinct lens so the parallel workers spread out and break fixation, rather than crowding the same obvious idea. Each entry is a name and a one-line prompt the generator follows.

## SCAMPER (Osborn's checklist, mnemonized by Eberle)

Best run as a rotation over an existing thing; assign one verb per generator for maximum spread.

- **Substitute** - what part, material, person, rule, or resource can you swap for something else?
- **Combine** - what ideas, features, processes, or people can you merge into something new?
- **Adapt** - what can you borrow from another product, context, or industry and adapt to fit?
- **Modify / Magnify / Minify** - what can you change, exaggerate, or shrink in size, shape, or feel?
- **Put to another use** - who else could use this, or what else could it be used for?
- **Eliminate** - what can you remove, simplify, or strip away without breaking the core?
- **Reverse / Rearrange** - what if you ran it backwards, or rearranged the order, layout, or parts?

## Lateral thinking (de Bono)

- **Provocation (PO)** - make a deliberately absurd or impossible statement about it, then mine it for usable ideas.
- **Random entry** - pick a random word, object, or image and force a connection to the problem.
- **Challenge** - ask "why does it have to be this way?" about each accepted feature, to escape the lock-in.
- **Concept extraction** - name the underlying concept behind an existing solution, then find new ways to deliver that concept.

## Analogy and nature

- **Analogical transfer** - find another industry that solved a structurally similar problem, and adapt how they did it.
- **Biomimicry** - ask how nature performs this function, and emulate the organism's strategy.

## First principles and assumptions

- **First-principles rebuild** - strip to fundamental truths, ignore how it is normally done, and reason a solution up from scratch.
- **Assumption reversal** - list every assumption you hold, reverse each, and force the reversed version to work.
- **Worst possible idea** - generate the worst, silliest ideas you can, then flip each into its opposite or mine it for a hidden good idea.
- **Reverse brainstorming** - ask "how could I cause this problem or achieve the opposite?", then reverse those answers into solutions.

## Constraints

- **Constraint removal** - imagine the biggest limit (budget, physics, time, legality) has vanished; now what would you build?
- **Constraint addition** - solve it with one-tenth the budget, by tomorrow, or with the key resource removed.

## Perspective and role

- **Rolestorming** - adopt a specific person, competitor, or archetype and generate the ideas they would propose.
- **Extreme users** - design for the most extreme user (a power user, a total novice, an extreme need); their fit reveals hidden ideas.
- **Figure storming** - channel a specific expert, historical figure, or a child, and frame the problem as they would.

## Future and framing

- **Future-back** - imagine it is ten years from now and it works perfectly; describe how, then work backward to what makes it possible.
- **What-if / blue-sky** - ask as many wild "what if?" questions as possible, suspending all feasibility judgment.
- **How might we** - reframe the problem as a "how might we?" question at the right altitude (see `brief.md`) to launch generation.
- **Starbursting** - put the idea at the center and generate questions around Who, What, When, Where, Why, How to surface angles.

## Combination and volume

- **Morphological analysis (Zwicky box)** - break the problem into independent parameters, list options for each in a matrix, then combine one option per parameter into novel hybrids.
- **Forced association** - force a connection between two unrelated things and build the idea the bridge implies.
- **Crazy Eights** - sketch eight distinct ideas in eight minutes; volume and speed beat perfecting the first one.
- **Brainwriting (6-3-5)** - write a few ideas, pass them on, build on or diverge from what the previous person wrote.

## Assigning lenses

One lens per generator. For a wide field, dispatch several generators in parallel on contrasting lenses (for example: a SCAMPER verb, an analogy, a constraint, an assumption reversal, and a first-principles rebuild). The provocation and random-entry lenses deliberately inject randomness and are the highest-leverage for breaking fixation.
