# Ideation method - diverge then converge

The structure every run follows, and why the team generates in isolated parallel workers. Read before dispatching. The one standard is generate wide, select fair.

## Diverge then converge (the structure)

The load-bearing rule across every serious ideation framework: generation and judgment are separated in time. Generate first, criticize second; mixing them collapses both.

- **Osborn-Parnes Creative Problem Solving (CPS).** Each stage runs a divergent phase (generate many options) then a convergent phase (select the most promising). The separation of the divergent and convergent phases is a core principle of CPS.
- **Design Council Double Diamond.** Two connected diamonds, each a diverge then converge: Discover then Define (the problem), Develop then Deliver (the solution). You cannot define until you have discovered, nor deliver until you have developed.
- **IDEO and Stanford d.school design thinking.** Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test, run as non-linear modes. Ideate is the divergent, judgment-free mode; synthesis is convergent.

## Osborn's four rules (Applied Imagination, 1953)

1. **Defer judgment** - no criticism or evaluation while generating.
2. **Go for quantity** - many ideas; quantity breeds quality.
3. **Welcome wild ideas** - "it is easier to tone down a wild idea than to think up a new one."
4. **Combine and build** - hitchhike on and recombine others' ideas.

## Why parallel independent generation wins (the evidence)

Face-to-face verbal group brainstorming underperforms the same number of people ideating alone and then pooling their ideas (nominal groups). Three mechanisms cause the loss:

- **Production blocking** (the dominant cause): only one person can speak at a time, so while waiting their turn people forget, suppress, or are distracted from their ideas (Diehl and Stroebe, 1987, 1991).
- **Evaluation apprehension**: fear of being judged makes people withhold ideas, which directly undermines the defer-judgment rule.
- **Social loafing / free-riding**: diffused responsibility in a group lowers individual effort.

A meta-analysis found interactive groups produce roughly 25 percent fewer ideas than nominal groups of the same size (Mullen, Johnson and Salas, 1991). Brainwriting and the nominal-group technique win precisely because members generate independently and in parallel, then pool: this eliminates production blocking, reduces evaluation apprehension, and curbs conformity (Paulus and Nijstad).

This is the direct justification for the team's method: **fan generation out to isolated parallel workers**, each deferring judgment, going for quantity, and welcoming wild ideas, then **converge to pool, combine, and select**. The Idea Generators never see each other's ideas while generating; the tournament decides, not the loudest voice.
