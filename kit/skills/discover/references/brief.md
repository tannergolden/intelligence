# Framing the brief and capturing an idea

How a challenge is framed determines the quality of the ideas. Frame before you generate. Read in Phase 0.

## How Might We (HMW)

Reframe the challenge as a "how might we?" question. HMW was coined by Min Basadur (out of the Creative Problem Solving Institute and his work at Procter and Gamble) and popularized by IDEO and the Stanford d.school. The word "might" defers judgment and opens possibilities, where "can" or "should" imply a verdict.

The altitude test: an HMW must be broad enough to admit many solutions yet narrow enough to give traction, and it must not contain a baked-in solution. Worked example:
- "How might we redesign the submission-drafting process?" is too broad (no starting point).
- "How might we make it quick to check work for mistakes?" is too narrow (it presumes the solution is error-checking).
- "How might we support users to efficiently draft submissions they are happy with?" is the right altitude.

Too narrow is the more common failure: a narrow HMW locks you onto one solution before generation begins. The checklist for a good HMW: based on a real problem or insight, tracks a desired outcome, written positively, broad enough for many ideas, and suggests no solution.

## Generate several HMWs by reframing

From one problem, vary the goal to get several HMWs, then generate against the strongest (the d.school reframing lenses):
- amp up the good, remove the bad, explore the opposite, question an assumption, go after the adjective, identify unexpected resources, create an analogy, play against the challenge, change the status quo, break the problem into pieces.

## Frame before ideate

Jumping straight to solutions severely constrains innovation, and most problem-solving that fails does so because the problem was under-examined. So restate the challenge several ways first (frame-storming before brainstorming), and reserve judgment and solutions for later. A wrong frame produces the wrong ideas, beautifully generated.

## Brief altitude

A brief is neither a vague theme nor a pre-baked solution. One clear challenge per run. The Double Diamond formalizes this: Discover and Define exist so the team understands, rather than assumes, the problem before Develop generates many answers to a well-articulated question.

## The idea record (lightweight, reusable, rankable)

A captured idea stays lightweight until vetted. Fields (the full schema and scoring are the Discovery team's method in this skill's `agents/Discovery.md`, scaffolded by `templates/idea-record.md`):
- a stable `id` (never reused; the dedup and lineage key),
- a short `title` and a one-sentence `statement`,
- the `lens` (or HMW) that produced it,
- a cheap `ice` score for intake triage and an `elo` from the tournament,
- its `lifecycle` (raw, shortlisted, developed, promoted, parked, killed),
- `parents` for lineage (what it evolved or combined from) and `serves` for the venture, project, or topic it is for.
